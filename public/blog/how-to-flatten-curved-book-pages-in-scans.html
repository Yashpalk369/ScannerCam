How to Flatten Curved Book Pages in Scans

By Scanner.cam Editorial Team · Updated 8 Nov 2026 · 6 min read
Quick answer

Flatten curved book pages in scans by photographing from a little further back, keeping the page as vertical as you can, and letting the scanner's perspective correction straighten the rest. A soft weight on the page and a straight-down camera angle fix most gutter curl without ever pressing the book flat.

Pages curve because a book is not a flat sheet: near the middle of a thick book, each page bends down into the gutter where it meets the spine. That bend makes lines of text arc, shrinks the inner margin, and blurs the words closest to the binding. The instinct is to press the book flat — but that is exactly what cracks spines. The better fix is a mix of shooting technique and software correction, and it works on almost every book. This is the technique deep-dive: for the gentle overall method, see how to scan book pages without damaging the spine.

Why pages curve — and what actually flattens them

A page curves in two directions: down into the gutter at the inner edge, and sometimes up at the outer edge if the book is thick. Perspective correction in a scanner straightens the image by remapping the photo as if it were taken square-on, so a gentle curve disappears completely. What it cannot fix is a page that is badly folded over, or text that is out of focus because part of the page is much closer to the camera than the rest. So the goal is not a perfectly flat page — it is a page curved so gently that the correction has something to work with.

The shooting fix: distance, angle, and height

  1. Step back a little. Photographing from 30 to 40 cm away instead of 15 cm makes the curve look flatter in the photo, because the difference in distance between the page edge and the gutter matters less. You lose a little resolution, but you gain a much straighter page.
  2. Hold the phone directly above the page. A straight-down shot keeps both edges the same distance from the lens. Even a small tilt makes one side of the page larger and the curve worse.
  3. Keep the page as vertical as you can. Open the book only as far as it opens comfortably, then support both covers so the page you are shooting sits as upright as possible instead of sloping down into the gutter.
  4. Check the gutter first. Zoom into the words nearest the binding on your first photo. If they are readable, your distance and angle are right — keep them for the whole session.
  5. Re-shoot the worst pages wider open. A few extra degrees of opening flattens the worst curl. What damages a spine is forcing it flat, not opening it a little more.

Weights and supports that hold a page flat

  • A beanbag or fabric pouch. Rest it gently on the page near the outer edge. It holds the page down without pressing the spine and moulds itself to the curve.
  • A rolled towel under the covers. Supporting both covers levels the book, so the open page sits flatter on its own.
  • Your fingertip at the very edge. One finger on the outer corner, out of the text block, is enough for a stubborn page. Keep fingers out of the text and out of the light.
  • Never a clamp, a heavy book, or your full hand. Hard pressure creases the page and stresses the binding — the exact damage this technique exists to avoid.

Let the scanner straighten what is left

After capture, Scanner.cam detects the page edges and applies perspective correction, which pulls the curved edges back into a rectangle. Crop to the text block afterwards: the crop removes the worst of the outer-edge curl from the final image. If a line near the gutter is still unreadable after correction, re-shoot that single page from a little further back rather than fighting it in editing. For a whole book, a steady batch workflow keeps your distance and angle consistent, which is what makes the correction reliable page after page.

When a page will not flatten

Some pages resist: a book that has been stored shut for years, a paperback with a tight glued binding, or a page that was folded. For these, photograph the page with the book resting on its side, supported, and accept a slightly angled shot that the scanner straightens. Or shoot the page in two halves — left and right of the gutter — and keep both halves in the same PDF. Never force the book, and never iron or dampen a page: heat and water damage paper permanently. If a page is too fragile to open at all, skip it and note the gap in your file name.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Pressing the book flat. The fastest way to crack a spine and the one mistake this whole technique exists to prevent.
  • Shooting too close. Close shots exaggerate the curve. Step back and let perspective correction do the work.
  • Tilting the phone. A tilted shot makes one side of the page bigger and the curl harder to correct.
  • Using flash on glossy pages. Flash glare is worst exactly where the page curves, hiding the text you most need.
  • Skipping the gutter check. The inner margin goes soft first. Check it on the first page, not the fiftieth.

Frequently asked questions

How do I flatten curved book pages in a scan?
Photograph from a little further back with the phone directly above the page, support the covers so the page sits upright, and let the scanner's perspective correction straighten the rest. A soft weight on the outer edge helps stubborn pages.

Can an app fix a curved page after I photograph it?
Yes, up to a point. Perspective correction flattens gentle curves well, but it cannot recover text that is out of focus or folded over. The shooting setup matters more than the software.

Should I press a book flat to scan it?
No. Pressing a book flat cracks the spine and creases pages. Photograph it from above with the book open only as far as it opens comfortably.

Why is the text near the gutter blurry?
The page curves down into the binding there, so it is further from the camera and out of focus. Step back, shoot straight down, and open the book a few degrees wider.

What do I do with a page that will not lie flat at all?
Photograph it with the book resting on its side, or shoot it in two halves — left and right of the gutter — and keep both in the same PDF. Never force, iron, or dampen the page.
