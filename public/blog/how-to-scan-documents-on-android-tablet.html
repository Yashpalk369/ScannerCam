How to Scan Documents on an Android Tablet

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 21 Oct 2026 · 5 min read
Quick answer

Android tablets don't have a single Apple-style scanning app — but they don't need one. The Google Drive app , which comes preinstalled on most Android tablets, has a full document scanner built in: it detects page edges, straightens captures, stacks multiple pages, and saves searchable PDFs.

It works exactly the same on tablets as on phones, and you can even pin a one-tap scan widget to the Home screen. Here's the Drive route step by step, plus a no-install browser alternative for watermark-free PDFs.

Your three options at a glance

  • Google Drive scanner (built in). No downloads on most tablets — the Drive app's scanner captures, cleans, and saves multi-page PDFs to your Drive.
  • Drive scan widget (fastest repeat scans). A Home-screen widget that opens the scanner in one tap and files the result in a folder you pick.
  • Browser scanner (no accounts). Scan in Chrome without signing in or uploading anything — best for documents you don't want in the cloud.

Method 1: scan with the Google Drive app

Google documents this flow in its own Drive help pages for "Android phone and tablet", and the steps are the same on both:

  1. Open the Google Drive app and sign in (or stay signed in — most tablets are).
  2. Tap the + (New) button in the bottom-right corner, then choose Scan. On newer Drive versions you may see a camera icon in the bottom bar instead — it opens the same scanner.
  3. Point the tablet's camera at the document. A blue outline appears around the detected page edges — that's the crop boundary. In Auto capture mode the photo fires when the page is steady; tap the shutter button to switch to Manual if you prefer to trigger each shot yourself.
  4. Review the preview. Crop or rotate if the edges need tightening, and apply a filter — Black & White for plain text, Color when stamps or highlights matter. There's also a cleanup tool for smudges and stray fingers.
  5. Tap the + (Add) button to scan the next page. Pages stack in order with thumbnail previews, and you can recapture or reorder any of them before saving.
  6. When the document is complete, tap Done (or the arrow), give it a filename — or accept the suggested title — choose .pdf or .jpg format, pick the Drive folder under Location, and tap Save.

The finished scan lands in your Drive as a searchable PDF, synced and available on every device signed into that account.

Add a one-tap scan widget to the Home screen

If you scan often, the widget skips the app-opening dance entirely:

  1. On the Home screen, long-press an empty spot (or open the Widgets panel — the route varies slightly by tablet brand).
  2. Find the Drive scan widget and drag it onto the Home screen. You may be asked to pick an account.
  3. Choose the folder scanned documents should land in (or create a new one), then tap Select.

From now on, one tap on the widget opens the scanner, and the finished PDF files itself into your chosen folder. Receipts, school letters, warranty cards — in and filed in seconds.

Method 2: scan in the browser — nothing to install or sign into

Drive scans live in Google's cloud, which is convenient but not always what you want — sensitive IDs, contracts, and medical forms are better kept off servers entirely. The browser route avoids that:

  1. Open Scanner.cam in Chrome on the tablet.
  2. Allow camera access when asked.
  3. Frame each page with the on-screen guides, capture, drag the corner handles to square it up, apply a document filter, and add more pages.
  4. Export a multi-page PDF — it downloads to the tablet, no account, no upload, no watermark.

Tips for cleaner scans on a big screen

  • Use landscape when framing. A tablet in landscape gives the viewfinder a wider, steadier frame for A4 and US Letter pages — less cropping, less distortion.
  • Steady the weight. Tablets are heavy; rest your elbows on the table or set the tablet on a stand and slide the paper underneath for the steadiest captures.
  • Contrast the background. Drive's blue edge outline locks on fastest with white paper on a dark surface — a dark tablecloth or folder works.
  • Light evenly. Overhead fluorescents cause banding and glare; a window's diffuse light (or two lamps from opposite sides) keeps pages flat-lit.
  • Check filters before saving. Drive applies its defaults, but a quick pass through the filter and crop tools on page one sets the look for the whole batch.
  • Split-screen while you work. Android tablets can run Drive alongside a notes or email app — scan in one half, reference the PDF in the other without switching apps.
  • Mind the account. Drive saves to whichever Google account is active — double-check you're scanning into the right one before a batch of work documents lands in a personal Drive (or vice versa).

FAQ

Does Google Drive scan documents on Android tablets?
Yes. The Drive app's built-in scanner works on both Android phones and tablets — tap + (New) → Scan (or the camera icon) and follow the capture flow. No extra app needed.

What format do Google Drive scans save in?
You choose when you save: .pdf for multi-page documents or .jpg for single images. PDFs from Drive are searchable.

How do I add more pages to a scan in Google Drive?
After each capture, tap the + (Add) button and scan the next page. Pages stack in order with thumbnail previews, and you can recapture or reorder them before tapping Done.

Where do Drive scans get saved?
In the Drive folder you pick under Location before saving — or the folder you assigned to the Drive scan widget. The scan syncs to every device signed into that Google account.

Can I scan on an Android tablet without installing anything?
Yes. Open a browser-based scanner like Scanner.cam in Chrome, allow camera access, and scan straight to a watermark-free multi-page PDF — no installs, no sign-in, and your pages never upload anywhere.
