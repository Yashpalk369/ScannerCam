How to Scan a Driving License for Online Verification

By Scanner.cam Editorial Team · Updated 6 Nov 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

To scan a driving license for online verification, capture both sides flat and glare-free, keep the photo and text sharp, and export a single PDF under the portal's size limit. A clean scan passes automated checks the first time; a blurry or cropped one gets rejected.

Banks, car-rental companies, insurers, and government portals all ask for the same thing: a readable copy of your driving license, front and back, that their verification system can actually read. The license is small, laminated, and reflective, which makes it one of the hardest documents to photograph well — glare across the photo, a cropped corner, or a thumb over the expiry date are the usual reasons a verification fails. Get the capture right and the rest is routine.

Scan your driving license in 6 steps

  1. Clean the card. Wipe fingerprints and dust off both sides with a soft cloth. Smudges on the laminate catch light and blur the text underneath.
  2. Put it on a dark, matte surface. A dark desk or a sheet of dark paper gives the camera contrast to find the card's edges, and matte surfaces do not reflect light back at the lens.
  3. Turn off the flash and use side light. Overhead light and flash bounce straight off the laminate. A lamp to one side, or daylight from a window, keeps the photo and text visible.
  4. Scan the front first, then the back. Keep the whole card in frame with a small margin around it — no cropped corners, no fingers in shot. Check the photo, name, and license number are sharp before moving on.
  5. Combine both sides into one PDF. Most portals want a single file. Front page first, back page second, in that order.
  6. Check the file size. Many verification portals cap uploads at 2–5 MB. If your PDF is larger, lower the scan quality slightly or compress it — never send a file the portal cannot accept.

Getting both sides right

The front of the license carries the photo, name, date of birth, and license number — the details the verification system reads first. Make sure the photo is not washed out by glare: tilt the card a few degrees until the reflection moves off the face. The back carries the categories, expiry date, and any restrictions. These are often printed smaller, so check them at full zoom after scanning; if the small text is unreadable, re-scan closer rather than hoping the portal will manage. If your license has a hologram or a chip symbol, do not worry if it looks odd in the scan — verification systems expect that.

File size and format

PDF is the safest format for license verification: it keeps both sides in order and prints exactly as scanned. JPG is accepted by some portals, but then you must upload two files and the order can get mixed up. Keep the scan in colour — black-and-white scans hide security features and make the photo harder to match. As a rule of thumb, aim for a PDF under 5 MB; if the portal states a lower limit, match it. And never send a scan at a resolution so low that the text turns to mush — readable text matters more than a small file.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Scanning with flash. Flash on laminate creates a white hotspot that wipes out the photo or the license number. Use ambient or side light instead.
  • Cropping a corner. Automated checks look for the full card outline. A missing corner reads as a tampered document.
  • Uploading only the front. Most verifications need both sides. Sending one side guarantees a rejection and a second upload.
  • Scanning an expired license. Check the expiry date before you upload. An expired license fails verification no matter how good the scan is.
  • Sending the scan over an insecure channel. Upload it through the portal itself, not by email or chat, and only to a service you trust with identity documents.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to scan both sides of my driving license?
Yes, almost always. The front proves who you are; the back proves the license is valid, what you are allowed to drive, and when it expires.

Why does my license scan keep getting rejected?
The usual causes are glare over the photo, cropped edges, blurry text, or a file that is too large. Re-scan in side light, check every edge is in frame, and confirm the size limit before uploading again.

Is a photo of my license enough, or do I need a proper scan?
A proper scan is better: edge detection squares the card up and evens out the lighting. A casual photo often has perspective distortion that automated checks reject.

What file size do verification portals accept?
Most accept up to 2–5 MB per file, but limits vary. Check the portal's upload page before you scan — it usually states the limit next to the upload button.

Is it safe to upload my driving license online?
Only to a service you trust, through its own secure upload page. A driving license is an identity document: never send it by unencrypted email, post it on social media, or upload it to a random site.
