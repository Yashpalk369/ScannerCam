How to Scan Documents With Google Drive's Built-In Scanner

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 20 Oct 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

Google Drive — the app already on most Android phones — hides one of the best free document scanners available. Open Drive, tap the camera icon, and you get automatic edge detection, multi-page capture, enhancement filters, and clean PDF export straight to your cloud storage.

What Drive's scanner gives you

  • Automatic document capture. Point at a page and Drive detects the edges and captures it — you can also tap the shutter manually or switch between auto and manual capture.
  • Multi-page documents. Scan page after page and Drive compiles them into a single document — contracts and forms stay together.
  • Enhancement filters. Clean up greys and shadows with colour modes (including black-and-white for text-heavy pages), plus crop, rotate, and reorder tools.
  • Smarter recent versions. Google's refreshed scanner adds batch capture, duplicate detection, and Auto Best Frame — it picks the sharpest image when several frames are captured.
  • Import from your gallery. Already photographed a page? Import it from the gallery inside the scanner and Drive converts it into a proper scan.
  • PDF or JPEG output. Save the finished scan as a PDF or JPEG, with Drive's AI suggesting a filename you can keep or change.

Step-by-step: scan a document in Google Drive

  1. Open the Google Drive app and sign in if needed.
  2. On Android, tap the camera icon in the bottom-right corner (older app versions: tap + New → Scan). On iPhone and iPad: tap Add (+) → Scan (or the camera icon).
  3. Position the document inside the frame. A blue outline shows where the scan will be cropped.
  4. Let auto-capture fire, or tap the shutter button yourself.
  5. Adjust the scan: drag the corners to fix the crop, rotate if needed, and pick an enhancement filter.
  6. For more pages, tap the + icon and keep scanning — each page is added to the same document.
  7. When done, tap the arrow / Next, rearrange pages if needed, then choose PDF or JPEG.
  8. Accept the suggested filename or type your own, pick the Drive folder, and tap Save / Upload.

Make Drive scans look professional

  • Light first, filter second. Even lighting near a window beats any post-capture filter. Filters rescue decent captures; they don't rescue dark, blurry ones.
  • Use Black & White for text documents. Contracts, letters, and forms come out dramatically cleaner — smaller files and sharper print.
  • Keep colour for what needs it. Certificates, stamps, signatures, and colour-coded forms should stay in colour — the B&W filter can wash out red ink and seals.
  • Check every page before saving. Zoom into the smallest text on each page in the preview. If one page is soft, rescan just that page while the document is still in front of you.
  • Turn off auto-capture for tricky pages. Glossy certificates and dark documents trigger early or misfires — manual capture with a steady hand wins.

Drive vs a private browser scanner: pick the right one

  • Use Google Drive when you want the scan stored in your cloud, searchable, shareable, and backed up automatically — perfect for receipts, invoices, and everyday paperwork you don't mind in your Drive.

FAQ

Is Google Drive's document scanner free?
Yes — it's included in the free Google Drive app on Android and iOS. There's no premium tier or watermark on scans.

Can Drive scan multiple pages into one PDF?
Yes. After each capture, tap the + icon to keep adding pages. When you finish, Drive compiles everything into a single PDF (or JPEG) document.

Do Drive scans upload to the cloud?
Yes — that's the point of Drive's scanner. The finished PDF is saved to your Google Drive account, which backs it up and syncs it across devices. For documents you don't want in the cloud, scan in a browser tool like Scanner.cam instead, where nothing uploads.

Where is the scan button in Google Drive?
On Android it's the camera icon in the bottom-right corner (older versions: + New → Scan). On iPhone/iPad it's Add (+) → Scan at the bottom right. The web version of Drive doesn't have the scanner — use the mobile app.
