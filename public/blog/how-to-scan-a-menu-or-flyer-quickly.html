Menus and flyers are the documents you meet once and need later: a takeaway menu taped to a wall, a restaurant menu you want to share with friends, a flyer for an event you might attend. A quick scan beats a blurry photo — the text is straight, readable, and saved as a proper file instead of disappearing into your camera roll.

Scan a menu or flyer in 3 steps

  1. Open Scanner.cam in your browser. No download, no signup — it opens straight to the camera. This matters when you are standing in front of a menu and want the scan done before the moment passes.
  2. Point and capture. Hold the phone over the page so the whole menu or flyer fills the frame. The scanner finds the edges and straightens the page for you. If the page is on a wall, step back until all four corners are in view.
  3. Check, save, and share. Zoom in and read a line or two — prices and phone numbers are the parts that matter most. Save it as a PDF, then share it to a friend or file it for later.

When one photo is enough

Most menus and flyers are a single page, and a single-page scan is all you need. A few habits make that one shot count:

  • Fill the frame. The closer the page, the sharper the text. Move in until the page nearly touches the edges of the screen — small text at the bottom of a menu is the first thing to go blurry.
  • Keep the phone parallel to the page. A tilted shot makes one side of the menu larger than the other and the text harder to read. If the menu is on a wall, hold the phone at the same height as the centre of the page.
  • Mind the light. A menu under a bright lamp or in a shop window often has a glare spot. Shift your position until the glare moves off the text, or ask for the menu to be moved into shade.

Menus: save them for later

A scanned menu is more useful than a photo because you can actually find it again:

  • Name it by place, not by date. spice-house-menu.pdf beats scan-2026-11-12.pdf every time you search for it.
  • Keep a menus folder. One folder for takeaway menus, restaurant menus, and café boards means dinner decisions take seconds, not a scroll through thousands of photos.
  • Share the PDF, not a screenshot. A PDF prints cleanly and reads well on any phone. Send it to the group chat and everyone is looking at the same prices.

Flyers, posters, and noticeboards

Flyers and posters follow the same quick-scan pattern, with two extra wrinkles:

  • Large posters need distance. Step back until the whole poster fits in the frame, then check the small print — dates, phone numbers, and ticket prices — at full zoom. If the small print is unreadable, take a second close-up of that corner and save both pages in one PDF.
  • Glass and lamination glare. Noticeboards behind glass reflect everything. Stand slightly to one side so your reflection moves off the text, and never use flash — it bounces straight back into the camera.
  • Multi-page flyers. A folded leaflet is just a small multi-page document: scan each panel in order and save them as one PDF so the story reads correctly.

Frequently asked questions

Can I scan a menu with my phone without an app?
Yes. Open Scanner.cam in your phone's browser, point the camera at the menu, and tap to capture. The page is straightened and saved as a PDF — no app to install and no account to create.

How do I scan a menu on a wall?
Step back until all four corners fit in the frame, hold the phone at the same height as the centre of the page, and watch out for glare from overhead lights. If the small print is unreadable, take a close-up of that section as a second page.

Is a scan better than a photo of a menu?
Yes. A scan straightens the page, evens out the light, and saves the result as a PDF with a proper file name, so you can find it later and share it. A photo stays crooked, often has glare, and gets lost in your camera roll.

Can I scan a flyer behind glass?
Yes, with care. Stand slightly to one side so your reflection moves off the text, turn off the flash, and shoot in the shade if you can. If the glare will not move, take the flyer out from behind the glass or photograph it from a different angle.

What format should I save a scanned menu as?
PDF. One file per menu, named after the place, is easy to search, share, and print. If you only need the text, run OCR on the scan and paste the menu into a notes app — but keep the PDF as the original.
