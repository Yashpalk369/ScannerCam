How to Scan a Prescription to Order Medicine Online

By Scanner.cam Editorial Team · Updated 10 Nov 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

To scan a prescription to order medicine online, capture the full prescription — doctor, patient, medicines, dosage, and signature — in one clear, well-lit scan, and upload it as a PDF or JPG as the pharmacy asks. A blurry dosage or a cropped signature is the most common reason an online pharmacy rejects an upload.

Online pharmacies need to read your prescription the way a pharmacist at a counter would: every medicine name, every dosage, and the doctor's signature must be legible. The upload takes a minute when the scan is right — and a rejected upload means starting the order again. This guide covers how to capture a prescription the pharmacy will accept first time.

What pharmacies check on your prescription

Requirements vary by pharmacy and by country, so always follow the instructions on the pharmacy's own upload page. Most online pharmacies check the same things:

  • The doctor's details: name, registration number, clinic letterhead, and signature — all visible and legible.
  • The patient's name and date: the prescription must be for you (or the person you are ordering for) and recent enough for the pharmacy to accept.
  • Every medicine and dosage: drug name, strength, quantity, and how often to take it — with nothing cropped out.
  • The full page: all four corners, both sides if the prescription continues on the back.

Scan the prescription clearly

A prescription is usually a single page, often handwritten. The handwriting is the hard part — your scan has to make it as easy to read as the original.

  1. Use good, even light. Daylight near a window beats a yellow lamp. Avoid shadows across the handwriting and avoid flash, which washes out ink.
  2. Lay the prescription flat on a dark, plain surface so the edges are easy to see and the page does not curl.
  3. Capture the whole page, all four corners included. A cropped signature or a missing line at the bottom is a rejection.
  4. Scan in colour. Colour keeps the ink, the letterhead, and any stamp readable. Black and white can blur handwritten notes.
  5. Zoom in and read it yourself before uploading. If you cannot read a dosage in your own scan, the pharmacist cannot either — re-scan.

Upload it the way the pharmacy asks

Most online pharmacies accept a JPG or a PDF upload, and many let you take the photo directly inside their app or website. Check three things before you tap upload:

  • File format: JPG or PDF, as the pharmacy specifies. A PDF is the safer choice when the prescription has more than one page.
  • File size: most upload forms cap files at a few megabytes. If your scan is too large, compress it rather than re-scanning at lower quality — and check the text is still legible afterwards.
  • One file per prescription unless the pharmacy asks otherwise. If two prescriptions are for the same order, keep them as separate files with clear names, for example prescription-blood-pressure-2026-11.pdf.

Keep your prescription private

A prescription is a medical record. Upload it only through the pharmacy's own website or app — never through a public link, a social-media message, or an unknown seller. Check that the pharmacy is a licensed, registered business before you share anything, and keep a copy of the scan for your own records so you can reorder or show it to your doctor later.

Common reasons uploads get rejected

  • A cropped signature or date. The pharmacy cannot verify a prescription it cannot fully read.
  • Blurry handwriting. Handwritten dosages need a sharp, well-lit scan — retake it in daylight if the first attempt is soft.
  • An expired prescription. Most pharmacies only accept recent prescriptions. If yours is old, ask your doctor for a fresh one.
  • Wrong file format or size. A 20 MB photo will bounce off most upload forms. Compress before you upload.
  • A screenshot of a photo. Screenshots lose quality. Upload the original scan or photo, not a screenshot of it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I scan a prescription to order medicine online?
Lay the prescription flat in good daylight, capture the full page in colour with all four corners visible, and check you can read every dosage and the doctor's signature before uploading. Save it as a JPG or PDF in the format the pharmacy asks for.

What should be visible on a prescription scan for an online pharmacy?
The doctor's name, registration number, and signature, the patient's name and date, and every medicine with its dosage — the whole page, with nothing cropped out.

Should I scan a prescription in colour or black and white?
In colour. Colour keeps handwritten ink, letterheads, and stamps readable, and most pharmacies prefer it.

Can I order medicine online with a photo of my prescription?
Yes — most online pharmacies accept a clear photo or scan, as long as the whole prescription is legible. Use the pharmacy's own upload page or app, and check the file format and size limit first.

Why was my prescription upload rejected?
Usually because part of the page was cropped, the handwriting was blurry, the prescription had expired, or the file was the wrong format or too large. Re-scan in daylight, check every line is legible, and upload the original file — not a screenshot.
