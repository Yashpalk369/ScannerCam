How to Scan Documents on an iPad

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 20 Oct 2026 · 6 min read
Quick answer

The iPad has one of the best built-in document scanners of any tablet, and it's hiding inside apps you already have: Notes and Files can both detect pages, straighten them, and save multi-page PDFs — no third-party app, no subscription, no watermark. Add an Apple Pencil and you can sign and annotate scans right on the glass.

Your three options at a glance

  • Notes app (most flexible). Scan into a note, keep pages alongside your text and checklists, then mark up or sign — best when the scan is part of a working document.
  • Files app (most direct). Scan straight into a folder as a standalone PDF — best when you just need a file to send or file away.
  • Browser scanner (most shareable). Scan in Safari and export a clean, watermark-free PDF — handy when the recipient isn't on Apple devices.

Method 1: scan documents in the Notes app

This is the route Apple documents in its own support pages, and it hasn't changed in years:

  1. Open the Notes app and create a new note (or open the one the scan belongs in).
  2. Tap the camera/attachment button (it may appear as a camera or paper-clip icon, depending on your iPadOS version).
  3. Choose Scan Documents.
  4. Hold the iPad over the page. In Auto mode the document gets highlighted and captured automatically once it's steady; tap Auto (top-right) to switch to Manual if you'd rather press the shutter yourself — better for small receipts, folded pages, or faint edges.
  5. After each capture, drag the corner circles to tighten the edges if needed, pick a filter (Color, Grayscale, Black & White, or Photo — black & white gives the cleanest text), then tap Keep Scan or Retake.
  6. Keep scanning pages — they stack into one document. When you're done, tap Save (the count shows how many pages you captured).
  7. The scan lands in your note as a PDF attachment. Tap it to crop, rotate, delete, or rescan individual pages, or share it via the share sheet — email, Messages, AirDrop, print, or Save to Files.

Method 2: scan documents in the Files app

If you don't want the scan living inside a note, the Files app skips straight to a file:

  1. Open the Files app and navigate to the folder where you want the scan to live.
  2. Tap the More button (three dots) and choose Scan Documents.
  3. Capture pages exactly as in the Notes flow — Auto or Manual, corner adjustments, filters, Keep Scan per page.
  4. Tap Done, choose the save location if asked, and tap Save. A standalone PDF appears in the folder.

From here, the file behaves like any PDF: rename it, move it to iCloud Drive or On My iPad, or share it anywhere.

Method 3: scan in the browser for a clean shareable PDF

Apple's scans are excellent, but they're wrapped in Apple's flow. If you want a plain watermark-free PDF with no Apple ID strings attached — say, for a government portal or a client — do it in the browser:

  1. Open Scanner.cam in Safari on the iPad.
  2. Allow camera access, frame each page with the on-screen guides, and capture.
  3. Square up the corners, apply a document filter, add the remaining pages, and export a multi-page PDF.

Everything is processed on the iPad itself — your pages never upload, which is the detail that matters for IDs and financial documents.

Mark up and sign scans with Apple Pencil

This is where the iPad pulls ahead of every other tablet. Open any scanned PDF (in Notes, Files, or anywhere else), tap the Markup button, and you can:

  • Sign — add a saved signature or draw one freehand with your finger or Apple Pencil, then drag it into place. Saved signatures live in Markup across apps.
  • Annotate — highlight, underline, or scribble margin notes with the Pencil's pen, marker, and highlighter tools.
  • Fill forms — tap text fields to type into them, or write by hand if you prefer.

A signed scan is the difference between "scan it, print it, sign it, scan it again" and just… signing it.

Tips for sharper iPad scans

  • Steady the big glass. An iPad is heavier than a phone — rest your elbows on the table and hold it with both hands, or lay the page on the floor and shoot straight down.
  • Contrast is king. White paper on a dark surface gives the edge detection something to grab onto; a white page on a white countertop confuses it.
  • Use diffuse light. Direct lamps cause glare on glossy receipts and laminated cards — a window's soft light or a ceiling bounce works better.
  • Check small print before saving. A scan can look fine at full-page size while account numbers or signatures blur when zoomed. Pinch in and check before you tap Save.
  • Black & White for forms, Color for stamps. Plain contracts and applications come out cleanest in black & white; keep Color when stamps, highlights, or seals matter.
  • Name it on the spot. "Scanned Document 12.pdf" in a crowded Files app is a future headache — rename while you still remember what it is.

FAQ

How do I scan documents on an iPad without downloading an app?
Open Notes, tap the camera/attachment button, and choose Scan Documents — or do the same from the More button in the Files app. Both are built into iPadOS; no downloads needed.

Where do iPad scans get saved?
Notes scans land inside the note as a PDF attachment; Files scans save as a standalone PDF in the folder you choose. From either spot you can share, print, or move the file via the share sheet.

Can I sign a scanned document on my iPad?
Yes. Open the scan, tap Markup, and add a saved signature or draw one with your finger or an Apple Pencil. You can also annotate, highlight, and fill forms the same way.

What's the difference between scanning in Notes and in the Files app?
Notes keeps the scan inside a note alongside your text — best when the scan is part of a working document. Files saves it directly as a standalone PDF in a folder — best when you just need a file to send or archive. The capture interface is the same in both.

Why are my iPad scans blurry?
Usually it's light or movement: scan under bright, diffuse light, steady the iPad with both hands (elbows on the table), and keep the page flat. If text is faint, rescan with the Black & White filter for crisper contrast.
