How to Scan Your Passport with a Phone

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 3 Nov 2026 · 5 min read
Quick answer

To scan your passport with a phone, lay the photo page flat in bright, even light, capture the page with all four corners visible, and save it as a clear PDF or JPEG. Avoid glare on the photo and the machine-readable zone at the bottom, because a reflection there is the most common reason passport scans get rejected.

Most passport scans are requested for a visa application, a travel booking, or an identity check, and the requirements are nearly always the same: the photo page must be fully readable, the photo must match the person, and the two lines of machine-readable text at the bottom must be crisp. A phone scan can meet all three, provided you slow down for the one page that matters and check it before you send it.

Which pages do you actually need?

For most applications, only the photo page is required — the page with your photograph, name, passport number, and dates. Some forms also ask for the signature page, any pages with visas or stamps, or the last page with emergency-contact details. Read the request before you scan: sending the whole booklet when only the photo page was asked for adds pages nobody will read, and missing a requested page sends you back to the start.

Scan each requested page as its own clear capture, then combine them into a single PDF in the order the form lists them. A single file in the requested order reads like the document itself and is much easier for a reviewer to work through than a set of loose images.

Scan the photo page step by step

  1. Open Scanner.cam in your phone's browser — nothing to install, and the scan is processed on your device rather than uploaded to a server.
  2. Place the passport open and flat on a plain, dark surface. A dark background helps the camera find the page edges, which matters because passport pages are small and pale.
  3. Work in bright, indirect light: near a window in daylight, or under a ceiling light. Avoid direct sun and overhead spotlights, which create hot spots on the laminated page.
  4. Hold the phone parallel to the page, directly above it, with all four corners of the photo page in the frame. Keep your fingers and shadow out of the shot.
  5. Tap the photo page on screen to focus on it, wait a second for the image to sharpen, then capture. Check the result at full zoom before moving on.
  6. Repeat for any other requested pages, then save the set as one PDF.

Beating glare on the laminated page

The photo page is laminated, and that laminate loves to reflect light back at the camera. If you see a bright patch over the photo or the machine-readable zone, tilt the passport slightly — a few degrees is usually enough — or move the light source to the side rather than directly above. Shooting at a slight angle is fine as long as the page edges stay visible; Scanner.cam straightens the perspective, but it cannot recover text hidden behind a reflection.

Never use flash on a passport page. The flash fires straight back off the laminate and wipes out exactly the details the reviewer needs. If the room is too dark without flash, move closer to a window or switch on a second lamp across the room instead.

Check the scan before you send it

  • Photo: your face is clear, not washed out or shadowed, and the photo matches the printed details.
  • Text: name, number, and dates are readable at full zoom, with no cut-off characters at the edges.
  • Machine-readable zone: both lines at the bottom are sharp, with no glare, fingers, or shadow over any character.
  • Format: PDF for a multi-page set, JPEG for a single page — whichever the form asks for. If no format is specified, PDF is the safest default.
  • File size: many portals cap uploads at a few megabytes, so compress a scan that comes out too large rather than cropping content out.

A note on privacy

A passport scan is one of the most sensitive documents you own, so treat it that way. Only scan your own passport, only send the scan to the service that asked for it, and avoid scanning or sharing it over public Wi-Fi or on a shared device. Because Scanner.cam processes scans on your device, the scan itself is never uploaded to a server by the tool — but once you send the file onward, its safety depends on the service that receives it. Delete spare copies from your phone's gallery once the application is accepted, and keep the original booklet safe.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to scan both sides of my passport?
Usually only the photo page is required. Scan additional pages only when the form asks for them, such as visa pages or the signature page, and keep everything in one PDF in the requested order.

Why was my passport scan rejected?
The most common reasons are glare over the photo or the machine-readable zone, cut-off page edges, and blur from shooting too close or in poor light. Retake the page flat, in indirect light, with all four corners visible, and check it at full zoom.

Should I scan my passport as a PDF or a JPEG?
Follow the form's instruction if it gives one. Otherwise, use PDF for a multi-page set and JPEG for a single page; PDF is the safest default because it opens the same way on every device.

Can I use a phone scan instead of a photocopy?
For most online applications, yes — a clear phone scan is accepted wherever a digital upload is requested. Some in-person appointments still ask to see the physical booklet, so always keep the original.

Is it safe to scan my passport with an online tool?
Prefer a tool that processes the scan on your device, so the image never leaves your phone during scanning. After that, only send the file to the service that requested it, and avoid public Wi-Fi for the upload.
