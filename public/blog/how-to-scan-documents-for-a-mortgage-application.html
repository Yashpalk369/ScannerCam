How to Scan Documents for a Mortgage Application

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 26 Oct 2026 · 6 min read
Quick answer

A mortgage application means handing your lender a stack of personal paperwork — identity documents, proof of income, bank statements, and proof of deposit — and every page needs to be legible, complete, and in the right order. Lenders reject blurry, cropped, or out-of-order scans, and each rejection adds days to your approval.

This guide walks through the documents most lenders ask for, how to scan each one properly, and how to package everything into one clean submission.

One important note before you start: every lender and every country runs its own checklist. Use this guide for the scanning and packaging part, and always work from the exact document list your lender or broker sends you — if they ask for a specific document, send that one, not a substitute.

The lender document checklist

Most mortgage applications ask for the same core set of documents. Gather them all before you start scanning so you can submit in one session:

  • Proof of identity: a government photo ID — national identity card or passport. Scan the front and the back of cards; for passports, scan the photo page.
  • Proof of income: recent pay slips (usually the last three to six months) and, if you are self-employed, tax returns and business accounts for the last one to two years.
  • Bank statements: typically the last three to six months for every account you hold, including savings accounts that show your deposit building up.
  • Proof of deposit: a savings statement, a gift letter if family helped with the deposit, or evidence of the sale of another property.
  • Proof of address: a recent utility bill, council tax bill, or bank statement showing your name and current address.
  • Existing debts and commitments: statements for loans, credit cards, or car finance the lender needs to factor into affordability.
  • Property documents: the purchase agreement or reservation form, and any valuation or survey paperwork your lender has already issued.

If any document is double-sided, scan both sides. A missing back page — especially on ID cards and statements — is one of the most common reasons mortgage uploads get bounced back.

Step 1: Scan in the order the lender lists them

Lenders and brokers review applications in checklist order, so scan and name your files in that same order. A typical sequence is:

  1. Identity documents first, then proof of address.
  2. Income documents next: pay slips, then tax returns or employer letters.
  3. Bank statements after income, in date order, oldest to newest.
  4. Deposit evidence, then debts, then property documents last.

Keeping the scan order matched to the checklist means the reviewer never has to hunt for a missing page, and if something is missing, you spot the gap before you submit rather than after.

Step 2: Get every page legible

Mortgage reviewers — human underwriters and automated checks alike — reject scans they cannot read. Get it right at capture time:

  • Flat and fully visible: lay each page on a plain, dark surface and capture the whole page, including all four corners. Numbers, dates, and account details near the edges must not be cut off.
  • Good, even light: scan near a window in daylight or under a bright room light. Avoid shadows from your phone and glare on laminated cards.
  • Colour, not black-and-white: submit colour scans. Stamps, signatures, and security features are part of what the lender checks, and greyscale can hide them.
  • Check before moving on: zoom in on each scan and confirm names, figures, and dates are readable. A blurry bank statement will be rejected and cost you days.

Scanning in your browser works well here: capture each page, straighten it, and export the whole set as one multi-page PDF without installing an app — and because processing happens on your device, your financial documents never upload to a scanner company's server.

Step 3: Name files so anyone can follow them

Clear filenames save your broker time and prevent mix-ups between similar documents:

  • Use plain names: 01-ID-front.pdf, 02-ID-back.pdf, 03-payslip-Jan.pdf, 04-bank-statement-3months.pdf. Numbering keeps the order obvious.
  • One document per file unless the portal asks for a combined upload. Merging your ID and your bank statement into one file confuses automated checks.
  • Keep the originals: save the clean scans in a folder on your device before uploading, so a rejected submission costs you a re-upload, not a re-scan.
  • Watch the file size: if a lender portal caps uploads, a standard scan exported as PDF is usually well under the limit; avoid sending huge raw photos.

Step 4: Submit safely

Mortgage documents are the most sensitive files most people ever upload, so treat the submission step seriously:

  • Upload only through your lender's or broker's own portal — never through a link in an unsolicited message or email asking you to "verify urgently."
  • Use your own device and connection where possible, not a shared or public computer, for the upload itself.
  • Delete stray copies: after a successful submission, remove duplicate scans from your downloads folder and messaging apps so the documents do not live in places you forgot about.
  • Never send mortgage documents over chat or social media to someone claiming to be your broker. Legitimate lenders collect them through their own secure upload flow.

If a submission is rejected, read the reason before re-uploading. Re-sending the same blurry scan will be rejected again; fix the specific problem — a cut-off corner, a missing back page, a statement that is too old — and then resubmit.

Common mortgage scan rejections (and the fix)

  • Blurry or dark pages: rescan in better light, hold steady, and zoom in to check the figures before submitting.
  • Corners or edges cut off: pull back so the full page is in frame, then crop to the document edges only.
  • Statement too old: lenders usually want the most recent months; check the date range on your lender's checklist and rescan a newer statement.
  • Only the front submitted: add the back page — most ID and statement checks require both sides.
  • Pages out of order: reorder the PDF so it follows the lender's checklist, and number the filenames.
  • Wrong file type: if the portal wants PDF and you sent photos, export the scans as a PDF first.

What documents do I need to scan for a mortgage application?
Typically proof of identity, proof of address, recent pay slips or tax returns, three to six months of bank statements, proof of deposit, and any existing debt statements — but the exact list varies by lender and country, so always work from the checklist your lender sends you.

Should mortgage scans be in colour or black-and-white?
Colour. Stamps, signatures, and security features are part of what the lender checks, and greyscale can hide them. Submit colour scans of every page, front and back where applicable.

Can I scan mortgage documents with my phone?
Yes. A phone camera with perspective correction produces scans lenders accept, as long as every page is flat, fully visible, well lit, and legible when zoomed in. Export as PDF rather than sending raw photos.

How do I keep mortgage scans private?
Scan with a tool that processes on your device, upload only through your lender's or broker's own portal, and delete stray copies from your downloads and messaging apps after a successful submission. Never send mortgage documents through chat or social media.

Why was my mortgage document scan rejected?
The usual reasons are blurry or dark pages, cut-off corners, a missing back page, a statement outside the lender's date range, or pages out of order. Read the rejection reason, fix that specific problem, and resubmit.
