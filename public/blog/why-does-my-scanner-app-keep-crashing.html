Why Does My Scanner App Keep Crashing?

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 29 Oct 2026 · 5 min read
Quick answer

Scanner apps usually crash because the phone runs out of storage or memory mid-scan, the app is out of date, or a corrupted cache trips the app when it tries to save. Work through the fixes in order — free space first, then update, then clear cache, then reinstall — and most crashes stop without losing your scans.

Start with storage: the most common cause

Scanning is storage-hungry. Each capture is a high-resolution image, and a multi-page document multiplies that before the app compresses everything into a PDF. If your phone is nearly full, the app can run out of room halfway through saving and close without warning. Check your free space first: on both iPhone and Android this lives in Settings under Storage. If you have less than about a gigabyte free, delete old videos, offload unused apps, or move photos to cloud storage before scanning anything important. Then try a single-page test scan — a blank sheet, never a sensitive document — to confirm the crash is gone.

Memory and large scans

Even with storage free, a phone with many apps open can run short of working memory, especially when scanning a long document at full resolution. Close other apps before a big scan, and avoid scanning dozens of pages in one session on an older phone. If crashes happen only on long documents but single pages work fine, split the job: scan in batches of ten pages or so, save each batch as its own PDF, and combine them afterwards. Lowering the app's image quality setting, if it has one, also reduces the load.

Update the app and the phone

Crashes that appear suddenly often follow an operating-system update that the scanner app has not caught up with, or a bug in an old app version that a newer release already fixed. Check your app store for a scanner update, and check Settings for a pending system update. Update the app first — it is the smaller change — then test again before updating the whole system, so you know which fix worked.

Clear the cache, not your scans

On Android, clearing an app's cache removes temporary files without deleting your saved scans; it is the safest "reset" available. Go to Settings, then Apps, find the scanner, choose Storage, and tap Clear cache. Do not tap Clear data on the first attempt — that can remove the app's saved documents and settings. On iPhone there is no per-app cache button, so the equivalent step is to offload the app (Settings, then General, then iPhone Storage, then the app, then Offload App) and reinstall it, which keeps your documents while replacing the app itself.

Permissions and camera problems

A scanner that crashes the moment you open the camera may have a revoked or half-granted permission. In Settings, then Apps (or Privacy on iPhone), confirm the scanner has camera permission and, if it saves to your photo library or files, storage permission too. Denying storage permission while the app tries to save is a classic crash trigger. Also check that no other app is holding the camera open — a video call or another camera app running in the background can conflict with the scanner.

When reinstalling is the right move

If free space, updates, cache and permissions have all been checked and the app still crashes, uninstall and reinstall it. Before you do, export or back up any scans stored inside the app, because uninstalling can delete app-private files that are not in your photo library. After reinstalling, scan a test page first. If the fresh install crashes on the same test, the problem is likely the phone itself — a failing storage chip or a system fault — and it is time for a browser-based alternative rather than another reinstall.

Use a browser scanner while you troubleshoot

You do not have to pause your paperwork while an app misbehaves. A browser-based scanner like Scanner.cam runs in Safari or Chrome with no install, no account and no app cache to corrupt, and it downloads the finished PDF straight to your phone. That makes it a useful diagnostic tool as well: if the browser scanner works fine on the same phone, the problem is the app, not the camera or the device.

Preventing the next crash

  1. Keep at least a gigabyte of free storage before a scanning session.
  2. Close heavy apps and avoid scanning very long documents in a single pass on older phones.
  3. Turn on automatic app updates, or check for updates when a crash first appears.
  4. Export finished PDFs out of the app regularly, so a future crash or reinstall never strands your archive.
  5. Test with a blank page after any fix, before scanning a document you cannot easily replace.

Why does my scanner app crash when I save?
Usually because the phone is out of storage or the app runs out of memory while building the PDF. Free up storage, close other apps, and try a single-page test scan before retrying a long document.

Will clearing cache delete my scans?
On Android, clearing cache removes temporary files only and should leave saved scans alone. Clearing data is different and can remove app-stored documents, so try cache first and back up scans before anything stronger.

Why does my scanner app crash only on long documents?
Long scans use much more memory and storage than single pages. Split the job into batches of about ten pages, save each batch separately, and combine the PDFs afterwards.

Should I reinstall a crashing scanner app?
Yes, but only after trying free space, updates, cache and permissions — and only after exporting any scans stored inside the app, since uninstalling can delete app-private files.

Can I scan without an app while mine is broken?
Yes. A browser-based scanner like Scanner.cam works in your phone's browser with no install, so you can keep scanning while you fix or replace the crashing app.
