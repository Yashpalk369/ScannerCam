How to Scan Documents With Your Phone Camera

Your phone camera can produce scans that look every bit as clean as a flatbed scanner — if you photograph the document correctly. Most "bad scan" problems (blurry text, shadows, crooked edges, washed-out ink) are photography problems, not camera problems. This guide covers the exact setup: lighting, angle, framing, and a few batch tricks that make multi-page documents fast.

The setup: before you take a single photo

  1. Flatten the document. Iron out curled pages, smooth creases with your palm, and if the page keeps curling, put it under a book for a few minutes first. A flat page reads far better than any auto-straightening filter can fix.
  2. Use a plain, dark backdrop. Lay white paper on a dark desk, a dark tablecloth, or even a dark floor. High contrast between the page and the surface is what lets scanning tools detect the page edges accurately.
  3. Find soft, even light. Bright indirect daylight near a window is ideal. Overhead room lights work too — just make sure light falls evenly across the whole page so one corner isn't glowing while another is dim.
  4. Clean your lens. A quick wipe with a soft cloth removes the fingerprints that cause haze. This one fix alone improves more "blurry" scans than any settings change.

How to photograph each page

  1. Hold the phone directly above, parallel to the page. Look down at the screen and check that all four edges of the document look parallel — tilt in any direction makes text stretch and shrinks one side. If your camera app has grid lines (most do, under camera settings), turn them on and align the page edges to the grid.
  2. Fill the frame. Get close enough that the document fills most of the viewfinder, leaving only a small margin of backdrop around each edge. Too far away wastes pixels; too close crops the corners. Tap the screen to focus on the text before shooting.
  3. Keep your shadow off the page. Your body standing between the light and the paper is the most common cause of dark bands across scans. Hold the phone to the side of the light path, or place the lamp behind your hands' position.
  4. Skip the flash. Flash creates a bright hotspot and hard shadows from curled edges. If it's too dark, move to a brighter spot rather than firing the flash.
  5. Check one page before shooting ten. Zoom into the first photo and read the smallest text. If it's legible, your setup is right — now shoot the rest. If not, adjust distance, light, or focus before committing to the batch.

Phone-camera tricks that improve scans

  • Burst mode for batches: shoot each page two or three times in quick succession and keep the sharpest. It takes seconds and saves you from discovering a blurry page 5 later.
  • Contrast filters help: after capturing, a "noir" or black-and-white contrast filter (built into most phones' photo editors) turns greyish office lighting into crisp black-on-white text. Scanner tools do this automatically — but it's a good fallback.
  • Keep pages in order as you shoot: stack finished pages face-down in a neat pile as you go, or number corners lightly with a pencil for long documents. Sorting 30 shuffled pages later is the real time cost of batch scanning.
  • Watch for glare on glossy paper: laminated cards and glossy brochures reflect light at an angle. Tilt the page slightly or move the light so the reflection falls off the page rather than across the text.

FAQ

Why are my phone scans blurry?
Almost always one of three things: a dirty lens (wipe it), movement while shooting (hold still a beat after tapping), or standing too far away so the text uses too few pixels. Tap-to-focus on the text and fill the frame before you blame the camera.

How do I avoid shadows in document photos?
Use light coming from the side rather than from behind you, and hold the phone just outside the light's path to the page. Two light sources from different angles (e.g. a window plus a room lamp) practically eliminate body shadows.

Do I need a scanner app on my phone?
Not necessarily. Your built-in camera plus careful technique produces very good scans. A dedicated scanner tool adds value when you need multi-page PDFs, automatic edge detection and perspective correction, or consistent results across a batch — and browser-based tools do it without an install.

What resolution should I photograph documents at?
Use your phone's default photo resolution; you want the text to occupy plenty of pixels. Avoid digital zoom entirely — walk closer instead of pinching to zoom, because digital zoom throws away detail.
