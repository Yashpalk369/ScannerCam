How to Scan Receipts for Expense Reports (Free, No App)

Paper receipts are the worst expense-report attachment: they're narrow, they curl, the thermal ink fades, and a blurry photo gets rejected by finance. The fix isn't a paid app — your browser is already a perfectly good receipt scanner. Here's a workflow that turns a pile of receipts into clean, legible PDFs in a few minutes, completely free.

Step-by-step: scan receipts in your browser

  1. Flatten the receipt: smooth it on the desk and press out the worst curls with the back of your phone for a few seconds.
  2. Open Scanner.cam on your phone's browser and tap Scan Camera. Allow camera access when asked.
  3. Place the receipt on a dark, contrasting background and frame it fully inside the guides — receipts are narrow, so get close rather than cropping later.
  4. Capture the photo, then drag the four corner handles to the receipt's edges. The 2.5x magnifying loupe helps you line up narrow edges precisely.
  5. Choose Magic Color to remove shadows and uneven lighting, or Clean B/W when the print is faint — high contrast makes amounts and dates readable.
  6. Repeat for each receipt, then reorder the pages by date or vendor.
  7. Tap Export to download a single watermark-free PDF you can attach to your expense report.

Everything runs locally in your browser — the receipt images never upload to a server, which matters when they show vendor names, card digits, or client details.

Tips for tricky receipts

  • Faded thermal receipts: scan them as soon as you can — heat, light, and friction keep fading thermal paper. If the print is already faint, Clean B/W mode with a contrast boost often rescues amounts that a photo would miss.
  • Long receipts: if a restaurant bill runs past one frame, capture it in two overlapping photos and place both captures in the same export, or fold it neatly and capture it flat in one shot.
  • Lighting: use bright, indirect light. Harsh overhead light bounces off glossy thermal paper and creates unreadable white spots.
  • Keep the originals until the report is approved — some companies still require physical receipts for audits.
  • Name your files: export one PDF per trip or month and name it clearly (e.g. expenses-september-2026.pdf) so finance doesn't have to guess.

Why not just use a scanner app?

Most receipt-scanning apps are fine until export time. CamScanner's free tier stamps a watermark on exported pages, and removing it permanently requires the paid subscription — listed at around $4.99/month or $49.99/year for premium access. For a receipt your employer has to read and archive, a watermark on every page looks sloppy. A browser-based scanner gives you the clean export without the subscription, the app download, or the account.

FAQ

Do I need OCR for expense reports?
Not usually. Finance and accounting tools accept image-based PDFs as attachments, so a clean, high-contrast scan is enough. If you want searchable text, run the exported PDF through any free OCR tool afterwards.

How should I organise scanned receipts?
One PDF per expense report or per trip, pages in chronological order. Add a cover page listing the totals if you're submitting many receipts at once.

Is it safe to scan receipts with a browser tool?
Yes, with a client-side tool like Scanner.cam — all processing happens on your device and nothing uploads to a server. Avoid tools that upload your receipts to "the cloud" by default, since receipts can contain partial card numbers and vendor details.

What if the receipt is too faded to read?
Try Clean B/W mode with maximum contrast before giving up. If the total is still unreadable, write the amount, date, and vendor by hand on the receipt, take the photo, and note the estimate to your manager — most expense policies accept a signed declaration for faded originals.
