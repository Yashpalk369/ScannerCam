How to Scan an ID Card Online (Front & Back)

Most ID verification forms ask for both sides of your card in a single file — the front with your photo and the back with the barcode or signature. You don't need a flatbed scanner or a paid app: your phone's browser can capture both sides crisply and merge them into one PDF in about a minute.

Step-by-step: scan front and back in your browser

  1. Find a dark, contrasting surface (ID cards are usually light-coloured, so a dark desk or cloth works well) and place the card face-up.
  2. Open Scanner.cam on your phone's browser and tap Scan Camera. Allow camera access when asked.
  3. Hold the phone parallel to the card and frame the front side fully inside the guides, leaving a small margin around the edges.
  4. Capture, then drag the four corner handles exactly to the card's corners so the perspective correction produces a straight rectangle.
  5. Flip the card and capture the back side the same way. Keep the same lighting so both pages look consistent.
  6. Reorder the two pages — front first, back second — then tap Export to download a single two-page PDF, ready to upload to whatever form asked for it.

Everything runs in your browser — the captures are processed locally and never sent to a server, which matters a great deal for a document like an ID card.

Tips for sharp, accepted ID scans

  • Kill the glare: ID cards and laminates reflect light badly. Shoot under soft, indirect light (near a window, not under a bare bulb) and tilt the phone slightly if you see a bright hotspot — a glare stripe across the photo or ID number is the most common reason scans get rejected.
  • Go parallel: hold the phone flat and centred over the card. The corner guides fix mild angles, but a parallel shot is always sharper.
  • Check the small print: zoom the preview and confirm the ID number, expiry date, and any barcode lines are crisp and unbroken before exporting.
  • Keep the resolution high: don't compress the PDF aggressively if the receiving form has a generous file limit — barcodes and fine text degrade first.
  • Same side up: if the card has a preferred reading orientation, rotate both pages the same way so the reviewer doesn't have to flip one.
  • Delete the photos afterwards: remove the camera-roll copies of your ID once the form is submitted — your phone gallery is a worse place to store ID copies than an encrypted drive.

A word on privacy before you scan

An ID card is about the most sensitive thing you'll ever scan: a full name, photo, ID number, date of birth, and address in one tidy package. That makes the choice of scanning tool matter more than usual. Some scanner tools upload your captures to their servers for "cloud processing" by default — once an ID image leaves your device, you have no real control over where it goes or how long it's kept.

Scanner.cam is built client-side: capture, cropping, and PDF export all happen in your browser, so the images never leave your device in the first place. That removes one whole category of risk. It's still on you to send the file only through the official channel that asked for it, and to delete local copies when you're done — no tool can protect you from uploading an ID to the wrong place.

FAQ

Why do forms ask for both sides of the ID?
The front carries the identity details; the back typically holds the barcode, MRZ lines, or signature panel that verification systems check. One two-page PDF keeps them together so nothing gets mismatched.

Is a phone scan accepted instead of a flatbed scan?
Almost always, yes — provided the result is sharp, glare-free, and uncropped. Government and bank portals routinely accept phone-camera scans; what they reject is blur and glare, not the device that captured it.

Is it safe to scan an ID card with a browser tool?
With a client-side tool like Scanner.cam, yes — processing happens locally in your browser and nothing uploads to a server. Avoid tools that send your captures to a cloud service, since ID images are prime targets if that service is ever breached.

The barcode side came out unreadable — what now?
Reshoot just the back with softer, more angled light and make sure the card is perfectly flat. If the barcode is physically worn, note it when submitting — most verification teams accept the readable side plus a manual review.
