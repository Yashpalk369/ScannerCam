How to Scan an ID Card Online (Front & Back, Free)

Most forms and verification processes don't want a photo of your ID — they want both sides of the card on a single clean page, legible and correctly sized. A blurry angled photo gets rejected; an app download for a one-time task is annoying. Your phone's browser can do this properly in about a minute: front and back captured straight, cropped to the card's edges, and exported as one PDF page, free and without installing anything.

Step-by-step: scan your ID card in your browser

  1. Place the ID card on a flat, dark, contrasting surface — a table or a dark folder. Remove it from any plastic sleeve, which causes glare.
  2. Open Scanner.cam on your phone's browser and tap Scan Camera. Allow camera access when asked.
  3. Hold the phone directly above the card, about 25–30 cm away, and frame the front of the card inside the guides. Keep the phone parallel to the card — tilt distorts the edges and small text.
  4. Capture the photo, then drag the four corner handles exactly to the card's corners. ID cards have small print, so line the corners up carefully; the 2.5x magnifying loupe helps.
  5. Flip the card and capture the back the same way. The back often has tiny text (issuing authority, barcode), so use bright indirect light and get close.
  6. Choose Magic Color for a natural look, or Clean B/W if the card's print is faint — high contrast keeps names and ID numbers readable after export.
  7. Arrange the two scans in the page order you want, then tap Export to download a single watermark-free PDF.

Everything runs locally in your browser — the ID images never upload to a server, which is exactly what you want for a document that carries your identity.

How to combine front and back on one PDF page

  • Side by side: capture each side as its own page, then export both pages into one PDF. Most scanners treat the front as page 1 and the back as page 2 — acceptable to nearly every verifier.
  • Single-page layout: if a form requires one image file, capture each side separately, then export a two-page PDF and print-to-image, or download the pages and combine them in any free image editor. Keep each side at the same scale so they look like one document.
  • Order matters: put the front first and the back second — the reverse order is the most common reason a manual reviewer bounces a submission.
  • Don't shrink the text: capture at full resolution and crop tight to the card's edges instead of leaving wide margins. Small ID numbers become unreadable if the card fills only half the page.

Privacy precautions before you share it

  • Only upload to the service that asked for it. Never email or message your ID scan to someone "for verification" outside the official form or portal.
  • Check where the file goes: a client-side tool like Scanner.cam processes everything on your device, so the images never leave your phone. Avoid scanners that upload to "the cloud" by default, where your ID could be stored indefinitely.
  • Add a watermark for extra protection: when an institution allows it, a note like "Submitted to X on 2026-10-08" across the scan deters reuse if the file leaks.
  • Delete the scan afterwards: remove the exported file from your phone and downloads once the verification is done, especially on shared devices.

Why not just use a scanner app?

Most scanner apps handle ID cards fine until export time, when the free tier stamps a watermark on the page or pushes a subscription. For an ID submission, a watermark across your identity document looks wrong and some reviewers reject it. A browser-based scan gives you the clean export without the app download, the account, or the subscription.

FAQ

Is it safe to scan my ID card with a browser tool?
Yes, with a client-side tool like Scanner.cam — all processing happens on your device and nothing uploads to a server. Don't use tools that upload documents to the cloud by default when scanning identity documents.

What format should I submit — PDF or JPG?
PDF is the safer default: it preserves quality, keeps the two sides in one file, and prints consistently. If the form only accepts images, export at high quality and check the text is readable at 100% zoom before uploading.

The back of my card has tiny text. How do I keep it legible?
Get closer when framing (the card should nearly fill the frame), use bright indirect light to avoid glare, and crop tight to the edges. Clean B/W mode boosts contrast on small print.

Do I need both sides?
Almost always, yes. Most banks, KYC checks, and government forms require front and back. Submitting only the front is the most common reason for a rejection.
