Can I Scan Documents Straight to Google Drive or Dropbox?

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 29 Oct 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

Yes — you can scan documents straight to Google Drive or Dropbox, and there are three ways to do it. Both services have a built-in scanner in their mobile apps that saves directly to your cloud folder, and any other scanner can save a PDF that you then upload to Drive or Dropbox.

The built-in route is fastest when you already live in one of those apps; the scan-then-upload route is more flexible when you want a scanner with no account.

Option 1: Scan inside the Google Drive app

Google Drive's own help pages describe scanning on Android as follows: open the Drive app, tap the camera button (on some versions, the plus button followed by Scan), capture the document, adjust the crop if needed, add more pages, then choose PDF or JPG, pick the destination folder, and tap Save. The finished file lands in the Drive folder you chose, as a searchable PDF, ready to open on any device signed in to the same account. The Drive website itself cannot start a scan, so this route needs the mobile app. If you already keep your files in Drive, this is the shortest path from paper to cloud.

Option 2: Scan inside the Dropbox app

Dropbox's help pages describe a similar flow in its mobile app: open the Dropbox app, tap the plus button, choose Scan Document, photograph the page, edit or add more pages, then adjust the save settings and tap Save. The scan is saved to your Dropbox account as a PDF, and Dropbox applies text recognition so the document becomes searchable by its contents. As with Drive, the scanner lives in the mobile app only — it is not available on the Dropbox website or desktop app — but once saved, the file can be organised from any device.

Option 3: Scan with any scanner, then upload

The third route suits anyone who does not want to scan inside a cloud app, or who wants a scanner that needs no account at all. Scan with a browser-based tool such as Scanner.cam — open the scanner in your phone's browser, capture the pages, and download the finished PDF. Then upload that PDF to Drive or Dropbox from the same phone: open the Drive or Dropbox app, use its upload or plus button, and choose the downloaded file, or share the PDF from your files app directly to Drive or Dropbox. This route takes one extra step, but it keeps scanning and storage separate: the scanner never sees your cloud account, and you choose the destination folder at upload time.

Which route should you choose?

  • Already in Drive every day: scan inside the Drive app. One app, one save, no file to move afterwards.
  • Already in Dropbox every day: scan inside the Dropbox app, for the same reason, plus searchable text on the saved PDF.
  • Sharing a device, or avoiding accounts: scan in the browser, then upload only the finished PDF. No sign-in is needed to scan, and nothing is stored in the cloud until you choose to upload it.
  • Scanning for someone else's folder: scan first, then upload or share the PDF to the right shared folder, rather than scanning directly into your own account and moving it later.

Keep cloud scans findable

Scanning straight to the cloud only helps if you can find the file later. Name scans the same way every time — date first, then what the document is, for example 2026-10-29-electricity-bill.pdf — and save into a folder for the year, client or project rather than the cloud root. Check the scan before you discard the paper: a file that synced to the cloud with a cut-off edge is no better than a bad photo. Finally, remember that a cloud scan is a copy on someone else's servers, so for highly sensitive documents, weigh convenience against the account's sharing settings and keep the original filing rules of whoever asked for the document.

Can I scan directly into Google Drive?
Yes, using the Google Drive mobile app: tap the camera button (or plus, then Scan), capture and adjust the pages, choose PDF and a destination folder, and tap Save. The Drive website cannot start a scan.

Can I scan directly into Dropbox?
Yes, using the Dropbox mobile app: tap the plus button, choose Scan Document, capture and edit the pages, adjust the save settings, and tap Save. The scan is saved to your Dropbox account as a PDF.

Do I need the Drive or Dropbox app to save scans there?
No. You can scan with any scanner, including a browser-based scanner with no account, download the PDF, and then upload it to Drive or Dropbox from your phone or computer.

Are scans saved to Drive or Dropbox searchable?
Google Drive saves scans as searchable PDFs, and Dropbox applies text recognition to scanned documents so their contents can be searched. Searchability depends on the scan being clear and legible.

Can I scan to Drive or Dropbox without an account?
Scanning itself needs no account if you use a browser scanner, but saving into Drive or Dropbox requires an account with that service, since the file has to live in your cloud storage.
