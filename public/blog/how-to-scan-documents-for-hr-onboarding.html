How to Scan Documents for HR Onboarding

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 29 Oct 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

Scanning documents for HR onboarding means turning a new hire's paper file into clean, correctly named PDFs that HR can file, verify and find later. Scan one document per PDF, check every page is readable, name files by employee and document type, and send them through the employer's approved channel.

Done that way, onboarding paperwork takes minutes per document instead of becoming a chase for missing pages.

The new-hire paperwork checklist

Exact requirements vary by employer and country, so treat this as a checklist to confirm against the employer's onboarding list, not as a legal specification. The documents that most commonly need scanning are:

  • Identity documents: national ID card or passport, front and back where applicable, scanned flat with all corners visible.
  • Right-to-work or eligibility proof: whatever the employer specifies — scan the full document, not a cropped photo of one corner.
  • Signed offer and contract: every page, including signature pages, in page order, as a single PDF per document.
  • Tax and payroll forms: completed and signed forms, scanned after signing so the signature is part of the file.
  • Bank details for payroll: only if the employer asks for a document rather than typed details, and only through the approved secure channel.
  • Qualifications and certificates: degrees, licences and training certificates, one PDF per certificate, so HR can verify each one separately.
  • References and background-check consent: signed consent forms, scanned in full with dates visible.

If a document is not on the employer's list, do not scan or send it. Extra personal documents in an HR file are a privacy risk, not helpfulness.

A scanning routine that HR teams can actually use

  1. Confirm the list first: ask HR which documents they need, in what format, and through which channel — email, HR portal or in-person handover — before scanning anything.
  2. Scan one document per PDF. A single file mixing an ID, a contract and a certificate is hard to file and harder to verify.
  3. Capture every page in order, including backs of ID cards and signature pages that are easy to forget.
  4. Check readability before moving on: names, numbers, dates and signatures must be legible, with no cut-off edges. Rescan immediately if anything is unclear.
  5. Name files consistently, for example ahmed-ali-id-card.pdf or ahmed-ali-signed-offer.pdf, so HR can file without renaming.
  6. Send through the approved channel only, then confirm receipt. Avoid sending sensitive documents through personal messaging apps or unencrypted email if the employer provides a secure portal.

Handling sensitive documents safely

Onboarding documents are among the most sensitive papers a person owns: ID numbers, signatures, bank details and addresses. Scan them on a device you control, in a private setting, and do not leave copies sitting in your camera roll, downloads folder or a shared device after they have been sent. If you use a browser-based scanner like Scanner.cam, scans are processed on your device and downloaded as PDFs with no account and no upload — but once downloaded, the file is in your hands, so move it to the approved channel promptly and delete local copies once HR confirms receipt. Never scan or share a colleague's documents without their knowledge.

Mistakes that delay onboarding

  • Photographing instead of scanning: tilted, shadowed photos with cut-off corners get rejected and have to be redone. A proper scan is accepted the first time.
  • Combining everything into one PDF: HR files documents separately; a mixed file has to be split by hand.
  • Missing the back of an ID: the back often carries the expiry date, address or security details HR needs. Scan both sides.
  • Scanning before signing: an unsigned form scanned early still needs a second scan after signing. Sign first, scan second.
  • Unclear filenames: "Scan 1" and "Document" force HR to open every file to identify it. A name that says whose document it is saves everyone time.

What documents do I need to scan for HR onboarding?
Typically identity documents, a signed offer or contract, tax and payroll forms, and any qualifications the employer asks for. Confirm the exact list with your employer first — requirements vary by employer and country, and you should not send documents they did not ask for.

Should each onboarding document be a separate PDF?
Yes. One document per PDF, named by employee and document type, makes filing and verification straightforward. A single mixed file has to be split apart by HR.

Do I need to scan both sides of an ID card?
Yes, where the card has information on both sides. The back often carries the expiry date or address details that HR needs to verify, so scan front and back in that order.

How should I send scanned onboarding documents to HR?
Through the channel your employer specifies — an HR portal, a secure upload link, or in person. Avoid personal messaging apps for sensitive documents, and confirm HR received the files before deleting your local copies.
