What to check before you commit to a scanner

  1. Export a test page first. Don't find out about a watermark after scanning 40 pages. Scan one page, export to PDF, and look at the corners and footer.
  2. Check where the file goes. Some apps upload to cloud servers by default. If you're scanning identity documents or contracts, a tool that processes everything on your device is safer.
  3. Count the signup steps. "Free" apps that demand an account, an email verification, and cloud sync before you can scan once aren't free of friction — they're free of charge. For a one-off scan, a no-signup browser tool is faster than an install.
  4. Check multi-page behaviour. A watermark on every page of a 10-page contract is ten problems. Confirm the policy covers PDFs, not just JPEG previews.
  5. Mind retired apps. Microsoft Lens was retired in 2026 — old articles still recommend it, but it can't create new scans. If a guide mentions it, check the date.

FAQ

Which free scanner app has no watermark?
Adobe Scan's free tier exports without a watermark (it does need a free Adobe account), Apple's Notes scanner and Google Drive's scanner are watermark-free for iPhone and Android users, and browser-based tools like Scanner.cam produce clean PDFs with no account at all. CamScanner's free tier adds a watermark to every export.

Can I remove a CamScanner watermark for free?
The reliable route is to re-scan the document in a watermark-free tool. Cropping or blurring the stamp works for your own documents, but re-scanning gives a cleaner result.

Is Microsoft Lens still a good free scanner?
Microsoft Lens was retired in early 2026 — removed from the app stores on 9 February and no longer able to create scans after 9 March. Microsoft's suggested replacement is the scanner built into OneDrive, which saves files to cloud storage rather than your device.

Are browser-based scanners safe?
A client-side one like Scanner.cam is — all processing happens on your device and nothing uploads. Be wary of browser tools that send your documents to a server by default, especially for identity documents.
