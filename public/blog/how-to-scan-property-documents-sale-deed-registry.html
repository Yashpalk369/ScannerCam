How to Scan Property Documents (Sale Deed, Registry)

By Scanner.cam Editorial Team · Updated 11 Nov 2026 · 5 min read
Quick answer

To scan property documents like a sale deed or registry papers, capture every page in colour at full resolution, stamps and signatures included, and save one named PDF per document. A legible scan protects you when the original is away — and it is the copy you will reach for when a buyer, bank, or authority asks for proof.

Property documents are the most valuable papers most people own. They are also large, often printed on legal-size paper, stamped, signed, and sometimes decades old. A bad scan — a cropped page, a missing stamp, a blurry registration number — can stall a sale or a loan. This guide covers how to capture them properly and store them safely.

What to scan in a property file

A complete property file usually contains several documents. Scan all of them, even the ones that look routine:

  • Sale deed / title deed: the main ownership document, every page, including the schedule of the property at the end.
  • Registry / registration papers: the registered copy with the sub-registrar's stamp and registration number.
  • Mutation / transfer records showing the property moved into your name.
  • Tax receipts and utility bills that support your ownership and address history.
  • Loan and clearance documents, including any no-dues certificate from the bank.

Scan large pages without cutting anything off

Sale deeds are often printed on legal-size or larger paper, with dense text and official stamps in the margins. The whole page matters — including the parts that look like decoration.

  1. Use a flat, well-lit surface and lay the page completely flat. A curled corner hides text and distorts the stamp.
  2. Capture the whole page, all four corners and all margins. Stamps and signatures often sit at the very edge of the page — a cropped margin can cut off the registration stamp.
  3. Scan in colour at full resolution. Colour preserves revenue stamps, seals, and signatures, and a high-resolution scan stays legible when a lawyer zooms in on a clause.
  4. Check the registration number and the stamp before moving on. Zoom in and confirm both are readable — they are the first things a buyer or bank will check.
  5. For oversized pages, scan in overlapping sections and stitch them together, or photograph the full page from directly above with the phone held steady. Never fold a deed to fit a scanner bed.

Handle old and fragile documents gently

Old deeds are often printed on thin paper that tears easily and fades in sunlight. Lay them flat, handle them by the edges, and scan them once — carefully — rather than repeatedly. If the paper is very fragile, photograph it from above instead of pressing it under glass. Scan the oldest documents first, while the ink is still readable.

Name and store the files safely

Property files are long-lived, so the naming should still make sense years from now:

  • One PDF per document, with all its pages inside: sale-deed-plot-42-2026.pdf, registry-papers-plot-42-2026.pdf.
  • Include the property identifier in the file name so files from different properties never get mixed up.
  • Keep the originals in a safe place — a bank locker or a fireproof box — and never hand the original deed to anyone except your lawyer or the registrar, and only when required.
  • Back up the scans in two places, for example an encrypted cloud folder and an external drive. Property documents are exactly the files you cannot afford to lose.
  • Share carefully. Send property scans only to the buyer, bank, or lawyer handling your case, and only through their own portal or email — never through a public link.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • A cropped stamp or signature. The stamp and signature are what make the document valid. If they are cut off, the scan is useless as proof.
  • Scanning only the first and last pages. Every page of a deed matters — the schedule, the witnesses, the middle pages with the terms. Scan them all.
  • Black-and-white scans. Revenue stamps and seals lose their meaning in grey. Scan in colour.
  • Generic file names. A folder of "scan001.pdf" files is a disaster during a sale. Name each file by document type and property.
  • Throwing away the original. A scan is a copy. The original deed is the legal document — keep it safe, always.

Frequently asked questions

How do I scan property documents like a sale deed?
Lay each page flat, scan in colour at full resolution with all four corners and margins included, and check the registration number, stamps, and signatures are legible before moving on. Save one clearly named PDF per document and keep the originals in a safe place.

Should property documents be scanned in colour?
Yes. Colour preserves revenue stamps, seals, and signatures — the parts that prove the document is genuine and registered.

Can I scan a sale deed with my phone?
Yes, as long as the whole page is in frame, the text is sharp, and nothing is cropped. For oversized pages, scan in overlapping sections or photograph the full page from directly above.

Do I still need the original deed after scanning it?
Yes. A scan is a convenient copy, but the original deed is the legal document. Keep it in a bank locker or a fireproof box, and share the scan — not the original — with buyers and banks.

How should I store scanned property documents?
One PDF per document, named by document type and property, backed up in two places — for example an encrypted cloud folder and an external drive — and shared only with the buyer, bank, or lawyer handling your case.
