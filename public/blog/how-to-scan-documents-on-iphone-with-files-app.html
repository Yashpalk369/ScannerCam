How to Scan Documents on iPhone With the Files App

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 17 Oct 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

The iPhone's Files app has a built-in document scanner that saves scans directly as PDFs into any folder — iCloud Drive, On My iPhone, or even a linked Google Drive or Dropbox folder. Pick the destination first, tap Scan Documents , and each page lands in that folder ready to attach or upload.

Why scan with Files instead of Notes

  • Destination-first scanning. Navigate to the folder where the PDF should live before you scan — the finished file is born in the right place, not orphaned in a note.
  • Standalone PDF. Files saves the scan as a real PDF file, ready to attach to emails, upload to tax portals, or file away — no export step needed.
  • Same scanning engine as Notes. Automatic edge detection, perspective correction, and color/grayscale/black-and-white filters — everything Apple's Notes scanner does, in folder form.
  • Cloud-friendly. Scans can land directly in iCloud Drive or any third-party location you've connected to Files.
  • Nothing to install. It's built into iOS — no app, no account, no subscription, no watermark.

Step-by-step: scan with the Files app

  1. Open Files and navigate to the folder where you want the PDF saved (iCloud Drive, On My iPhone, or a connected cloud folder).
  2. Tap the More (…) button — the three-dot menu near the top right of the screen.
  3. Select Scan Documents. The camera opens in scan mode.
  4. Lay the document flat and hold the iPhone parallel to the page, roughly 8–12 inches above it, with all four corners in the frame.
  5. Watch for the yellow outline marking the detected edges. Let Auto capture fire once the image is stable — or tap the shutter button yourself for manual control.
  6. Drag the corners to fine-tune the crop, then tap Keep Scan. Repeat for additional pages.
  7. Tap Done, confirm the location, then tap Save — the PDF appears in your chosen folder.

Choose the right scan look

During scanning, tap the three circles at the top to pick how the scan renders: Color (the default, best for forms with stamps or logos), Grayscale, Black & White (crispest for plain printed text), or Photo. If you're in low light, tap the flash icon — it defaults to Auto and fires when the room is dim. Black & White usually gives the smallest file size and the sharpest text.

Get cleaner results from the Files scanner

  • Pick the folder first. Choosing the destination before scanning keeps the PDF from becoming a misplaced orphan file.
  • Shoot straight-on. Angled shots get perspective correction, but a straight-on capture preserves small print and dense tables best.
  • Light it evenly. Well-lit pages scan sharp; shadows and glare soften text.
  • Keep it steady. Brace your arms or rest your elbows on the desk — camera shake is the main cause of blurry scans.
  • Name it immediately. Long-press the PDF, tap Rename, and use a searchable pattern like "2026-10-18 Rent Receipt".

Files vs Notes vs Preview: which scanner to use

  • Files — best when you want a standalone PDF in a specific folder, ready to attach or upload.
  • Notes — best for quick scans you plan to bundle into a note and share from there.
  • Preview (iOS 26+) — best for scanning and then working with the PDF: renaming, annotating, filling, and signing.
  • Scanner.cam in Safari — best for a private, watermark-free PDF with cleanup filters, processed entirely on your device with nothing uploaded.

FAQ

Does the Files app scanner work on all iPhones?
The Files scan feature arrived with iOS 13 and works on any iPhone running that version or later. The steps are the same on iPad.

Where do Files scans save?
Wherever you choose at save time — the folder you were browsing in Files when you started, in iCloud Drive, On My iPhone, or a connected cloud service like Google Drive or Dropbox.

Can I scan multiple pages into one PDF?
Yes. Keep scanning pages with "Keep Scan" after each one, then tap Done — Files combines all pages into a single PDF.

Are the scanned PDFs searchable?
The scans are image-based PDFs by default, so text may not be selectable or searchable. If you need selectable text, run the PDF through an OCR step afterwards — or use a browser tool that adds cleanup and OCR to the pipeline.

Is scanning with Files private?
Yes — capture and processing happen on the iPhone itself. Files saves locally or to the cloud location you chose; nothing is sent to Apple beyond your normal iCloud sync.
