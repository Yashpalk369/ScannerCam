How to Scan Documents for a Job Application

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 25 Oct 2026 · 6 min read
Quick answer

Most job applications now ask you to upload your documents — CV, degrees, experience letters, and ID — through an online portal or by email. A tidy, legible scan pack makes you look organised; a pile of crooked phone photos does the opposite.

This guide shows you how to scan each document properly, assemble them into one clean pack, and send them in a format recruiters can actually open.

Before you scan anything, check what the employer actually asked for. Some want a single combined PDF, others want each document uploaded separately, and some only want the CV until later stages. Scan the full set once, then assemble it to match their instructions.

What to scan for a job application

The exact list varies by employer and country, but a typical application pack includes:

  • Your CV or résumé — if it only exists on paper, scan it; if it exists digitally, send the digital file instead of scanning a printout.
  • Degrees and diplomas — the certificate pages, including any transcript pages the employer lists.
  • Experience or employment letters — one per previous employer, if the posting asks for them.
  • Professional licences or certifications — only the ones relevant to the role.
  • National ID or passport page — only if the employer explicitly asks for it at this stage; do not volunteer identity documents unsolicited.

Do not scan or send originals you cannot replace, and never send documents the posting did not ask for. Extra pages slow down a recruiter and can raise privacy questions about why you shared them.

Step 1: Scan each document properly

Scan one document at a time, and check each scan before moving on:

  1. Lay it flat on a plain surface and capture the whole page, all four corners visible.
  2. Use good light — daylight near a window, or a bright room light. Avoid shadows across the text and glare on laminated certificates.
  3. Check readability: zoom in on the scan. Names, dates, grades, and stamps must be sharp and legible.
  4. Scan both sides when it matters: degree certificates sometimes carry grades, seals, or attestations on the back — if the back has content, scan it too.
  5. Keep colour: submit colour scans. Seals, stamps, and signatures are part of what an employer verifies, and greyscale can hide them.

Step 2: Assemble the pack in the right order

Order matters. A recruiter opening your pack should meet the documents in the order they expect:

  1. CV first — it is the document they will read first, so it goes first.
  2. Degrees and diplomas next, highest qualification first, in the same order your CV lists them.
  3. Experience letters after that, most recent employer first.
  4. Certifications and licences last, if included.

If the portal wants separate uploads, keep the same naming logic per file. If it wants one combined PDF, merge the scans in this order into a single file. Either way, the order inside your own folder should match, so you never have to rebuild the pack from scratch.

Step 3: Name files like a recruiter can use them

Recruiters download dozens of application packs. Clear filenames are a small kindness that also prevents your files getting lost:

  • Put your name in every filename: Ahmed-Raza-CV.pdf, Ahmed-Raza-BSc-degree.pdf, Ahmed-Raza-experience-letter-ABC-Company.pdf.
  • Use plain words, no spaces-as-chaos: hyphens or underscores, no special characters, no camera defaults like IMG_4821.jpg.
  • For a combined pack: Ahmed-Raza-application-pack.pdf — one file, your name, obvious contents.
  • Keep the originals folder: save the individual clean scans separately, so you can reassemble a different pack for the next application without rescanning.

Step 4: Mind file size and format

Application portals commonly cap uploads, and email attachments have practical limits too:

  • PDF is the safest format for application documents — it preserves layout on every device and is what recruiters expect.
  • Keep each file reasonably small: a scanned certificate page exported as a PDF is normally well under portal limits. If a file is huge, re-export it rather than sending a giant raw photo.
  • Check the portal's rules before uploading: some accept only PDF, some accept images too, and some cap the number of files. Match their rules rather than guessing.
  • Open the final PDF yourself before sending — every page, in order, legible. A merged file with a missing or upside-down page is easy to catch here and embarrassing to catch after sending.

Step 5: Send it properly

  • Through the portal: upload to the fields the employer labelled — CV to the CV field, certificates to the documents field. Do not dump everything into one field if the form gives you separate ones.
  • By email: attach the pack as a document (not as photos pasted into the message body), write a short cover note in the email itself, and use a clear subject line with the role title.
  • Double-check the recipient: an application sent to the wrong address is gone. Confirm the portal or email address from the original posting, not from a forwarded message.
  • Keep a copy of what you sent: save the exact pack you submitted, so you can answer questions about it in an interview without guessing which version went out.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Crooked, shadowy photos instead of proper scans — rescan in good light rather than sending a photo you already know is poor.
  • Missing back pages on certificates that carry grades or attestations on the reverse.
  • One giant photo dump instead of an ordered, named pack.
  • Sending ID documents nobody asked for — share identity documents only when the employer explicitly requests them, through their official channel.
  • Scanning a printout of a digital CV — if you have the digital file, send that; a scan of a printout is always worse than the original.

Should I combine all my documents into one PDF for a job application?
Only if the employer asks for a single file. If the portal gives you separate upload fields, use them — one document per field, clearly named. If it asks for one combined PDF, merge your scans in order: CV first, then degrees, then experience letters.

What format should scanned job application documents be in?
PDF, in almost every case. It preserves layout on every device, keeps multi-page documents together, and is the format recruiters and portals expect. Send images only if the portal explicitly asks for them.

Do I need to scan both sides of my degree certificate?
Scan the back too if it carries anything — grades, seals, attestations, or stamps. If the back is genuinely blank, the front alone is fine. When in doubt, include the back; an extra legible page never hurt an application.

Should I include my ID in a job application pack?
Only if the employer explicitly asks for it. Many employers check identity later in the process, after shortlisting. Volunteering identity documents early adds privacy risk for no benefit, so wait until they are requested.

How do I keep scanned certificates legible?
Scan flat in good, even light, keep the scan in colour, and zoom in to check names, dates, and stamps before you send. A scan you cannot read yourself is one a recruiter cannot read either — rescan it rather than hoping it passes.
