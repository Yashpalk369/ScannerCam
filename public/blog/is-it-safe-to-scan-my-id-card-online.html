Is It Safe to Scan My ID Card Online?

By Scanner.cam Editorial Team · Updated 6 Nov 2026 · 5 min read
Quick answer

Scanning your ID card online is safe only when you control where the file goes: a browser tool that processes everything on your device keeps your ID off other people's servers, while uploading it to an unknown site hands a copy of your identity to strangers. Check the tool before you scan, not after.

Your ID card is not just another document. It carries your full name, photo, date of birth, and a government-issued number — everything someone needs to pretend to be you. Banks, SIM providers, and freelance platforms legitimately ask for it, so most of us will scan an ID online at some point. The risk is not the scanning itself; it is where the image ends up. This guide explains the real risks honestly, the checks to run before you scan, and the workflow that keeps your ID as private as possible.

The honest risks

  • Identity theft. A clear scan of your ID card plus a few public details is enough to open accounts, take out loans, or register services in your name. This is the main risk, and it is real — identity fraud built on stolen ID scans is one of the most common forms of online fraud.
  • Data breaches. Every server your ID is uploaded to can be breached. A company that stores thousands of customer ID scans is a target, and breaches of such databases happen regularly.
  • Resale and reuse. Shady services collect ID scans and sell them on. A site offering a "free" scan or verification with no clear business model should make you suspicious about what it does with the files.
  • Copies you cannot delete. Once your ID scan sits on someone else's server, you cannot be sure it is ever truly deleted — even if the site says it is. Copies in backups and caches can linger for years.

Checks to run before you scan

  1. Check where the processing happens. Prefer a tool that processes the scan on your device — in your browser — so the image never leaves your phone or computer. If a tool uploads your ID to its server, that is a copy you no longer control.
  2. Check the site itself. Look for a real privacy policy that says what happens to uploads, a contact page, and an HTTPS address. A scanner site with no policy page and no company details is a red flag.
  3. Ask whether the upload is really needed. If you only need a PDF to send to your bank, you do not need a site that stores it. Scan locally, download the file, and upload it yourself through the bank's own portal.
  4. Check what the requesting service actually needs. Some verifications need the full card; others accept a cropped version or a masked number. Send the minimum that satisfies the request.
  5. Never scan an ID on public Wi-Fi for an upload-based tool. If the file has to travel to a server, do it on a network you trust.

The safer way to scan an ID

Use a browser-based scanner that processes everything on your device, like Scanner.cam: open the page, capture the card, and download the PDF — nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, and there is no account holding your details. Then upload that PDF yourself, through the official portal of the bank, government service, or platform that asked for it. That way only one organisation ever holds a copy: the one you chose. After the upload is confirmed, delete the scan from your downloads folder and your phone's gallery, and do not keep spare copies in email or cloud drives. If the service lets you, ask how long it keeps your ID and whether you can request deletion after verification.

What never to do

  • Never email your ID scan unencrypted. Standard email is not a secure channel for identity documents.
  • Never post your ID on social media — not even a "look what I got" photo of a new card. The number and photo are all a fraudster needs.
  • Never upload your ID to a site you found through an ad or a random link. Check the address and the company behind it first.
  • Never scan your ID for a stranger. No legitimate employer, landlord, or buyer needs your ID card sent over chat before you have met them or signed anything.
  • Never keep old ID scans in your phone gallery. If your phone is lost or stolen, the gallery is the first place a thief looks.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to scan my ID card with an online scanner?
It is safe if the scanner processes the image on your device and never uploads it. It is not safe if the tool sends your ID to its own servers — then you are trusting that company with your identity.

Can someone steal my identity from a scanned ID card?
Yes. A clear scan gives a fraudster your name, photo, date of birth, and ID number, which is often enough to pass basic verification checks. That is why you should only share scans with organisations you trust, through their official channels.

How can I tell if a scanner website is legit?
Look for a privacy policy that explains what happens to your files, a contact page, HTTPS, and a clear statement that processing happens on your device. No policy page, no company name, and vague promises are warning signs.

Should I delete my ID scan after uploading it?
Yes. Once the verification is confirmed, delete the scan from your device, downloads, and gallery. Keeping spare copies only increases the number of places your identity can leak from.

Is a scan of my ID card as valid as the physical card?
For most online verifications, yes — a clear scan is what portals expect. For in-person checks, the physical card is still required. A scan never replaces the card itself.
