How to Scan Receipts With Your Phone's Built-In Camera (No App)

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 31 Oct 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

To scan a receipt with no app, open your phone's built-in scanner — iPhone Notes or Files, Google Drive, or your camera app's document mode — lay the receipt flat in good light, capture it so all four edges are visible, and save it as a PDF. The whole job takes under a minute.

Receipts are the worst documents to photograph casually: thermal paper curls, fades, and reflects light, and a crooked photo of a long receipt is often unreadable by the time expenses are due. The good news is that the scanner you need is already on your phone, and it handles the hard parts — edge detection, straightening, and contrast — automatically. This guide covers the built-in route on iPhone and Android, the receipt-specific tricks that matter, and how to file scans so expense claims go through first time.

Find the scanner already on your phone

On iPhone, open Notes, start a new note, tap the camera button, and choose Scan Documents; or open Files, long-press in a folder, and choose Scan Documents. Both save a clean PDF straight into your phone. On Android, open Google Drive, tap the camera button, and choose Scan, or open your camera app and look for a document or scan mode — Samsung, Pixel, and OnePlus phones all include one, with OnePlus and Oppo calling theirs Doc Scanner. If your camera app offers no document mode, a browser scanner such as Scanner.cam does the same job with no install: open the site, capture the receipt, and download the PDF.

Whichever route you pick, the workflow is identical: capture, check, save. Do not worry about which one is "best" — for a single receipt, the one already on your phone is best, because it is the one you will actually use at the till.

Capture a receipt that stays readable

Flatten the receipt first. Thermal paper curls stubbornly, so press it under a book for a few seconds or hold the ends flat with your fingertips outside the frame. Lay it on a dark, flat surface so the white paper's edges stand out, and work in bright, even light — daylight near a window is ideal, while a direct flash often blows out the faint thermal print.

Hold the phone parallel to the receipt and fit the whole slip in the frame, including the shop name at the top and the total at the bottom — expense teams reject scans with the total cut off. Capture, then zoom into the preview and read the total and the date yourself. If either is soft or unreadable, retake it now; a receipt that fades further over the following weeks will not get more legible later.

Long receipts, faded print, and crumpled paper

For a receipt longer than your screen, capture it in two overlapping halves and keep both in one PDF, in order — or fold it carefully at a blank gap between items and capture each half separately. Never cut a receipt in half to make it fit; expense policies usually require the complete original.

Faded thermal print is the hardest case. Increase contrast if your scanner offers an enhancement or black-and-white mode, and capture it the same day you receive it — thermal print fades with heat and light, and a scan taken months later may be blank. For a crumpled receipt, flatten it as best you can and accept a slightly imperfect scan; a readable total and date matter more than a perfectly rectangular page.

File it so expenses go through first time

Name the file the way your expense system wants it: "2026-10-31-taxi-airport-42.pdf" beats "IMG_4821.jpg" and saves everyone a search. Save one PDF per receipt, and file it the same day — a camera roll full of unscanned receipts is where claims go to die. If your employer uses an expense app, upload the PDF there; if they want email, attach the PDF rather than pasting a photo into the message body, where it gets compressed and shrunk.

Keep the paper receipt until the claim is paid. Most policies accept the scan, but if a claim is queried, having the original ends the argument. Once paid, you can shred routine receipts — but keep tax-relevant ones for as long as your local rules require.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to download a scanner app for receipts?
No. iPhone Notes or Files, Google Drive, and most camera apps already include a document scanner that saves clean PDFs.

Why do my receipt photos keep getting rejected?
Usually the total or date is cut off, blurred, or washed out by flash. Reframe to include all four edges, use even daylight, and zoom into the preview to read the total before saving.

Can I scan a long receipt that does not fit on screen?
Yes. Capture it in two overlapping halves, in order, and keep both pages in one PDF. Do not cut the receipt.

What if the receipt print has already faded?
Try a black-and-white or high-contrast enhancement, and scan in bright, even light. Faded thermal print rarely improves, so scan new receipts the day you get them.

Is a scanned receipt accepted for expenses?
In most workplaces, yes — a legible PDF scan is the standard submission. Keep the paper original until the claim is paid, in case it is queried.
