How to Scan Documents for Online Banking Verification

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 2 Nov 2026 · 5 min read
Quick answer

To scan documents for online banking verification, scan your government photo ID and proof of address in colour, with all four corners visible, and upload them only through your bank's official app or portal. A clean scan in PDF or JPEG, with every name, number, and date legible at full zoom, is what bank reviewers check first.

Never email scans to an address you were not expecting, and never upload them through a link in an unsolicited message.

Banks ask for these documents for the same reason every regulated financial service does: they must confirm you are who you claim to be before they open or fully activate an account. The documents themselves are familiar — a national ID card, passport, or driving licence, plus a utility bill or bank statement as proof of address — but the scan quality rules are stricter than for a casual photo, because a reviewer (or an automated check) has to read fine print reliably. This guide covers what to prepare, how to scan it, and the mistakes that cause rejections.

What banks usually ask for

Expect two categories. First, a government-issued photo ID: a national ID card, passport, or driving licence, current and unexpired, with your photo, full name, and ID number clearly visible. Second, proof of address: a recent utility bill, bank statement, or official letter showing your full name and residential address, matching the name and address on your bank application exactly.

Some banks also ask for a second document, such as a tax number document or an income proof, depending on the account type. Check the exact list in your bank's app or portal before you start scanning — scanning a document the bank did not ask for wastes your time, and missing one document stalls the whole application. If a document has two sides, such as an ID card, scan both sides; the back often carries the expiry date or address the reviewer needs.

Scan for legibility, not for beauty

Open Scanner.cam in your browser and scan on your device — your documents are processed locally and never uploaded to a scanner's server, which matters when the document in question is your ID. Lay the document flat on a plain, dark surface in even light, hold the phone directly above it, and capture all four corners with no fingers, shadows, or glare over text, photos, or machine-readable zones.

Colour matters here: scan in colour, not black and white, so security features, stamps, and photos reproduce faithfully. After capturing, zoom in and read the scan yourself. If you cannot read a number at full zoom, neither can the reviewer. Retake it. For a multi-page statement, keep the pages in order in a single PDF, and make sure the page showing your name and address is included — a statement cropped to show only transactions proves nothing about your address.

Format, size, and naming

  • Format: PDF for multi-page documents, JPEG or PNG for a single card. If the bank's upload field names a format, use exactly that one.
  • Size: check the upload field's limit before you submit — limits vary by bank and by field. Compress a scan that is too large rather than cropping content out.
  • Colour: always colour for ID documents; black-and-white copies are a common reason for rejection.
  • Completeness: all four corners visible, both sides where applicable, nothing cut off or covered.
  • File names: use clear names such as "id-card-front-back.pdf" and "proof-of-address.pdf" so you can find and re-upload them if asked.

Upload safely, and only in the right place

Upload through your bank's official app or website, reached by typing the address yourself or using the app you already have — not through a link in a text message or email, however official it looks. Banks do not ask you to verify documents by email attachment, and a request to do so is a strong phishing signal. If you are unsure whether a request is genuine, call the bank on the number on the back of your card and ask.

After uploading, keep the scans somewhere safe and private — an encrypted folder or a password-protected device folder, not a shared album or an open downloads folder. Delete any extra copies, such as test scans, so old ID images do not linger on your phone. If the bank rejects a document, read the reason carefully before resubmitting: a rejection for "illegible" needs a rescan, while a rejection for "name mismatch" needs you to check the details on your application, not a better photo.

Mistakes that delay verification

The usual rejections are avoidable: expired ID, black-and-white copies, cropped edges, glare over the photo, a proof-of-address document that is too old, and a name or address that does not match the application exactly. Double-check expiry dates before you scan — an expired ID is rejected no matter how perfect the scan is — and check that the address on your proof matches the address you gave the bank, including spelling.

One more: do not edit the scan. Do not adjust colours, sharpen aggressively, or touch up a photo. Reviewers and automated checks compare the document against expected security features, and edits — even well-meaning ones — can look like tampering. A clean, unedited scan is always the safest submission.

Frequently asked questions

What documents do banks need for online verification?
Usually a government-issued photo ID (national ID card, passport, or driving licence) and a proof of address (utility bill, bank statement, or official letter). The exact list varies by bank and account type, so check your bank's portal first.

Should ID scans be in colour or black and white?
Colour, always. Black-and-white copies hide security features and photos, and banks commonly reject them.

Do I need to scan both sides of my ID card?
Yes, if the card is two-sided. The back often carries the expiry date or address the reviewer needs, and a front-only scan is an easy rejection.

Is it safe to email my ID scan to the bank?
No. Upload only through the bank's official app or website. A request to send ID documents by email is a phishing red flag — verify it with the bank directly first.

Why was my bank verification rejected?
The most common reasons are an expired ID, an illegible or cropped scan, a black-and-white copy, an outdated proof of address, or a name or address mismatch with your application. Fix the specific reason given, not just the photo.
