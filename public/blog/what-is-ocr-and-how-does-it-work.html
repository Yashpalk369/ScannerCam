What Is OCR and How Does It Work?

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 3 Nov 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

OCR, short for optical character recognition, is technology that reads the text inside a picture or scan and turns it into selectable, searchable text you can copy and edit. It works by recognising letter shapes in the image, so a sharp, well-lit scan gives far better results than a blurry or shadowy one.

You have probably used OCR without noticing: copying text out of a photo on your phone, searching a scanned PDF for a word, or having a receipt app pull out the total. In each case, the starting point is just pixels — a picture of words — and OCR is the step that turns those pixels back into actual text your device can work with.

How OCR reads a page, in plain terms

OCR works in stages. First it cleans up the image: straightening a tilted page, boosting contrast between text and background, and removing speckles. Then it finds the text itself, separating lines of writing from pictures, borders, and blank space. Next it recognises the characters, matching each letter shape against patterns it has learned from thousands of examples of printed and handwritten text. Finally it reassembles those characters into words and lines, often using language knowledge to resolve ambiguous letters — telling a capital I from a lowercase l, for example, by what makes sense in context.

Modern OCR also handles layout: columns, tables, and headings are recognised as structure, not just as a stream of letters. That is why a good OCR result preserves paragraphs and lists instead of dumping every word into one block.

What makes OCR accurate — or inaccurate

  • Scan quality matters most. Sharp, evenly lit, high-contrast scans recognise cleanly; blur, shadows, and glare are the top causes of errors.
  • Print beats handwriting. Printed text recognises reliably. Handwriting varies from person to person, so results range from good for neat writing to poor for scrawl.
  • Fonts and languages matter. Common fonts in supported languages work best. Decorative typefaces, unusual scripts, and mixed languages in one document all lower accuracy.
  • Tables and numbers need checking. Figures, dates, and table cells are where errors hurt most, because a misread digit changes the meaning. Always proofread numbers in OCR output against the original.

From scan to searchable text: the workflow

  1. Scan the document with your phone, following the usual rules: flat, well-lit, all edges visible, checked at full zoom.
  2. Run OCR on the scan — Scanner.cam's image-to-text tool extracts text from an image in your browser, and many scanner tools offer a searchable-PDF option that embeds the recognised text invisibly behind the page image.
  3. Copy the text where you need it, or save the searchable PDF so the document can be searched later without retyping anything.
  4. Proofread the result, especially names, numbers, and dates, before you rely on it for anything important.

What OCR cannot do

OCR reads text; it does not understand it. It will not summarise a document, verify a signature, or know whether a figure is correct — those are separate steps, sometimes done by other tools after OCR has produced the text. It also cannot recover text that is not visible in the image: a cut-off edge, a covered line, or a reflection over a paragraph means those words are simply missing, and no OCR engine can invent them. When accuracy matters, the scan itself is the place to invest effort, not the settings afterwards.

Frequently asked questions

What does OCR stand for?
Optical character recognition: technology that converts pictures of text — scans, photos, screenshots — into selectable, editable text.

Can OCR read handwriting?
Sometimes. Neat, consistent handwriting recognises reasonably well, but varied or rushed handwriting is much harder, and results should always be checked against the original.

Why is my OCR result full of errors?
Almost always scan quality: blur, shadows, glare, or low contrast. Rescan the page flat and well-lit, and check the result again before blaming the OCR tool.

What is a searchable PDF?
A PDF that looks like a normal scan but carries an invisible layer of recognised text behind each page image, so you can search, select, and copy text from it.

Is OCR free?
Many tools offer free OCR for basic use, including browser-based options that process the image on your device. Paid tools typically add higher volume, more languages, or better handwriting support.
