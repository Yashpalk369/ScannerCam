Before you scan: get the contract ready

  • Count the pages first. Flip through and note the total, including schedules, exhibits, and appendices. A missed page is the most common contract-scan mistake.
  • Unstaple and flatten. Stapled pages curl and cast shadows; remove staples so every page lies flat. Iron out creases with your palm.
  • Keep everything in original order. Main agreement first, then schedules and exhibits, signature pages where they appear. Number the corners lightly with a pencil if pages are loose.
  • Watch for long paper. Some contracts print on US Legal (14-inch) paper. Frame carefully — a legal page shot as letter size loses the margins.

Signature pages and fine print

  • Check signatures and dates at 100% zoom. After capturing each signature page, pinch-zoom the photo and read the signature line, printed name, and date. A blurry signature block is a rejected filing.
  • Initials on each page: if the contract has per-page initials, capture each one — even blank-looking pages with a single initial in the corner.
  • Get close on dense clauses. Fill the frame with the page so small print occupies plenty of pixels. Avoid digital zoom; move the phone closer instead.

Redact before you share

Contracts often travel further than intended — to counterparties, agents, or filing portals. Black out account numbers, personal details, or sensitive clauses on the copy you share.

  • Redact copies, never the original. Keep your unredacted scan intact and apply blackout boxes only to the shared copy.
  • Make redaction permanent. Use solid black boxes baked into the page image, not annotations that can be peeled off. If in doubt, print-to-PDF the redacted version so nothing sits in a hidden layer.

Privacy: keep the whole thing client-side

A contract is confidential by nature — names, amounts, obligations. Some scanning tools upload your pages to their servers for processing, which puts that confidential material on someone else's infrastructure.

File naming and e-signature readiness

  • Name it like you'll search for it. Use a pattern such as PartyName-Contract-YYYY-MM-DD.pdf so the file is findable years later.
  • E-signature ready: a clean, straightened PDF imports directly into signing tools without rework. Keep one unsigned scan as your archive copy.
  • Share securely: send the PDF through the channel the other party specified, and check who else can access it before uploading to shared folders.

FAQ

Will a scanned contract be accepted?
Scanned PDFs are widely accepted for records, filings, and sharing — most portals and counterparties ask for PDF copies. For execution or registration requirements, check what the receiving party or registry needs before you send; some still require a wet-ink or certified original.

Can I scan just the signature page?
You can, but a complete scan is better: a signature page without the agreement it signs proves little. Scan the full document, then export a redacted or excerpted copy if only part of it needs to travel.

Is it safe to scan a contract online?
Yes, with a client-side tool: if all processing happens in your browser, the contract never leaves your device. Avoid scanners that upload pages to a server by default for anything confidential.
