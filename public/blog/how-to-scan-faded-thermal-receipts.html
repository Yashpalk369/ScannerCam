How to Scan Faded Thermal Receipts Before the Ink Disappears

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 3 Nov 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

To scan a faded thermal receipt, capture it now — before more ink disappears — laid flat under bright, even light, then boost the contrast on the scan so faint text turns readable again. Save the result as a PDF straight away, because the paper original will only keep fading.

Thermal print is heat-sensitive, so keep the receipt away from sunlight, heat, and plastic sleeves while you work.

Thermal receipts — the shiny, thin slips from shops, ATMs, and petrol stations — are printed without ink. Heat darkens a chemical coating on the paper, and that coating keeps reacting: light, heat, and even the plastic of a wallet sleeve fade the print over months. A receipt that is faint today may be blank next year, which is why scanning it is a rescue job, not a filing chore.

Handle the receipt gently before you scan

  • Do not iron it, laminate it, or leave it in a hot car — heat darkens the whole slip and can wipe out the text.
  • Do not store it pressed against printed paper or inside a plastic sleeve for long; both can accelerate fading.
  • If the slip is curled, lay it under a heavy book for a few minutes rather than forcing it flat — a curled edge throws a shadow across the scan.
  • Photograph the worst receipts first. Work from faintest to clearest so the most at-risk slips are saved even if you run out of time.

Scan for maximum readability

  1. Lay the receipt flat on a plain, dark background — dark paper behind a pale slip helps the camera find the edges.
  2. Use bright, even light from two sides if you can, and turn the flash off: direct flash bounces off the glossy thermal surface and blanks out lines.
  3. Hold the phone steady and parallel to the receipt, and fill the frame with the slip so small print gets as many pixels as possible.
  4. Scan in colour first, even if the print looks black — colour capture preserves faint brown or grey traces that a black-and-white filter would drop.
  5. After capture, switch the scan to grayscale or black-and-white and raise the contrast: faint text that was barely visible on the paper often becomes legible once the background is pushed to white.
  6. Check the scan at full zoom before you file it. If a total or a date is still unreadable, rescan that section closer up rather than accepting a guess.

Save it so it survives

Export the scan as a PDF named by date and shop — 2026-03-14-supermarket.pdf — and keep one copy in a cloud folder and one on your computer or an external drive. If the amount matters for expenses or tax, type the total and date into the file name or a short note alongside the scan while you can still read them; a year from now, even your own scan may be the only record left. The paper original can then go into an envelope in a cool, dark drawer — kept as a backup, but no longer the only copy.

Frequently asked questions

Why do thermal receipts fade at all?
Thermal printing uses heat-reactive paper instead of ink. Light, heat, humidity, and contact with plastics keep the chemical reaction going, so the print gradually fades — sometimes to blank — over months or years.

Can a scan recover text that is already invisible?
Sometimes partially: contrast enhancement can pull back faint traces the eye misses. But once the coating has fully faded, no scan can recover what is gone — which is why scanning early matters more than any filter.

Should I scan in colour or black-and-white?
Capture in colour, then convert to high-contrast grayscale or black-and-white afterwards. Colour capture keeps faint traces; the contrast pass afterwards is what makes them readable.

Does laminating a receipt preserve it?
No — lamination uses heat, which can darken or erase thermal print on the spot. Scan it first, then store the original flat in a cool, dark place.

How long do thermal receipts last?
It varies a lot with storage: a slip in a sunny car can fade in weeks, while one kept cool and dark may stay readable for years. Treat every thermal receipt as temporary and scan anything you might need later.
