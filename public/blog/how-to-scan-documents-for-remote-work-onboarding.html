How to Scan Documents for Remote Work Onboarding

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 31 Oct 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

To scan documents for remote work onboarding, gather everything your new employer asked for — ID, signed offer, tax forms, bank details, and certificates — scan each one as a separate clean PDF, name the files clearly, and send them only through the employer's approved channel. Keep the originals until your first payslip confirms everything was accepted.

Remote onboarding has no office scanner and no HR desk to hand paper to, so the quality of your scans is the whole first impression — and a rejected ID scan can delay your start date. The routine is simple once it is a habit: work from the employer's checklist, scan one document per PDF, verify every page, and send through the channel they specified, never by unencrypted email unless they explicitly ask for it. This guide covers the full workflow, including the privacy habits that matter when your most sensitive documents are involved.

Work from the employer's checklist

Start with the exact list your employer sent: typically a photo ID (front and back), a signed offer letter or contract, tax withholding forms, bank account details for payroll, proof of address, and any role certificates. Tick items off as you scan them, and ask HR promptly about anything unclear — a missing document discovered on your start date is far worse than one question asked a week early.

Note how they want the files: one PDF per document is the safest default, and most HR systems prefer it. If they specify a portal, use it; if they specify an encrypted upload link, use that link and nothing else. Never send ID scans through ordinary social messaging apps or personal email unless the employer explicitly instructs it.

Scan one document per clean PDF

Use your phone's built-in scanner or a browser scanner such as Scanner.cam, which needs no app and processes pages on your device. Scan in order, include every page — including the back of an ID card and any page carrying a stamp or signature — and check each page at full size before exporting. A cut-off signature line or a blurred ID number is the most common reason onboarding scans get sent back.

For forms you must sign, print, sign, and scan the signed pages; do not send an unsigned form and promise the signature later. If a form allows electronic signature, follow the employer's tool for that — a photo of a signature pasted into a document is not always accepted. Date every signature the way the form asks, and keep a copy of exactly what you sent.

Name, verify, and send through the approved channel

Name files consistently — "surname-id-front-back.pdf", "surname-offer-signed.pdf", "surname-bank-details.pdf" — so HR can file them without asking which attachment is which. Before sending, open each PDF and flip through it: right document, right pages, right order, nothing cut off. Then send through the approved channel only, and confirm receipt: a portal success message, an HR reply, or a read receipt, depending on the system.

After sending, do not delete anything. Keep your PDFs until your first payslip arrives and your HR record is confirmed — that is the point at which you know every document was accepted. If HR asks for a resend, you can answer in seconds instead of rescanning under deadline pressure.

Keep sensitive scans private

Onboarding documents are among the most sensitive files you will ever scan: ID numbers, signatures, bank details, and tax information. Prefer a scanner that processes pages on your device and never uploads them, and avoid scanning on shared or public computers. On your own phone, delete the camera-roll originals once the PDFs are saved, if your phone keeps both, and make sure the PDFs are not auto-uploaded to a shared album or a family cloud account.

Be cautious with what you share beyond the employer: never post onboarding documents, offer letters with salary details, or ID scans to social media, even partially redacted — a "new job" photo with a visible document edge has leaked more than one start date. If a document contains information the employer did not ask for, ask HR whether to include it rather than sending extra sensitive pages by default.

Frequently asked questions

What documents do I need for remote onboarding?
Typically photo ID, a signed offer or contract, tax forms, bank details for payroll, proof of address, and role certificates. Work from your employer's exact checklist, and ask about anything unclear.

Should I send one PDF or one file per document?
One PDF per document is the safest default and what most HR systems prefer, unless your employer specifies a combined file.

Can I scan onboarding documents with my phone?
Yes. Your phone's built-in scanner or a browser scanner produces clean PDFs with no app install, and on-device processing keeps sensitive pages off other servers.

Is it safe to email my ID scan to HR?
Only through the channel your employer specified. Prefer their portal or encrypted upload link, and never send ID scans through social messaging apps unless explicitly instructed.

How long should I keep my onboarding scans?
Until your first payslip confirms everything was accepted — and keep the originals longer for tax and employment records.
