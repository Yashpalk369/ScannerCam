How to Scan Documents on a Surface or 2-in-1 Tablet

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 20 Oct 2026 · 6 min read
Quick answer

A Surface tablet — or any Windows 2-in-1 — is a surprisingly capable document scanner. The rear camera points at the desk when you flip the device around, the big screen makes framing pages easy, and on some models the built-in Windows 11 Camera app has a document mode that straightens pages for you.

Wherever the built-in route falls short, you can scan straight in the browser instead — no installs, no accounts, nothing to pay for. Here are the three routes that work today, plus the tablet-handling tricks that make the scans come out sharp.

Your three options at a glance

  • Windows 11 Camera app (fastest, built in). If your model's camera supports it, the Camera app's document mode detects the page, straightens it, and saves a clean scan. Nothing to install.
  • Browser scanner (most universal). Open a web-based scanner in Edge or Chrome, allow camera access, and scan from any 2-in-1 — works regardless of whether the Camera app has document mode.
  • Microsoft 365 Copilot app (Microsoft's current pick). Microsoft retired its standalone Lens scanning app in late 2025 and now points users at the scanning feature inside the Copilot app.

Method 1: scan with the Windows 11 Camera app

First, set the tablet up to scan: detach the keyboard (or fold it behind the screen) so you can hold the device like a camera, with the rear camera facing the desk. Most Surface Pro and Surface Go models carry a rear camera with autofocus — Microsoft itself lists document scanning as one of its uses — and the same steps work on other brands' 2-in-1s.

  1. Open the Camera app from the Start menu.
  2. If it opens on the front camera, tap the switch camera icon so the rear camera is active.
  3. Look for a document or "scan" mode in the app's mode bar (often shown as a page icon). If you see it, select it — the app will detect page edges, straighten the perspective, and capture the page.
  4. Hold the tablet parallel to the page, steady it with both hands (elbows on the table help), and capture. Flip the page and capture again for multi-page documents.
  5. Captures save to your Pictures folder (Camera Roll) — rename them, then combine them into a PDF with Print → Microsoft Print to PDF if you need one file.

Honest caveat: document mode isn't on every camera — if the button simply isn't there, your camera doesn't support it, and no settings update will conjure it up. That's exactly what Method 2 is for.

Method 2: scan in the browser — nothing to install

The browser route sidesteps the Camera app entirely and works on every Surface, 2-in-1, or Windows tablet ever made:

  1. Open Scanner.cam in Edge or Chrome on the tablet.
  2. Allow camera access when the browser asks — pick the rear camera if it offers a choice.
  3. Frame each page using the on-screen guides. Capture, then drag the corner handles to square up the page, apply a document filter to clean up the text, and add more pages.
  4. Export a multi-page PDF. It downloads to your Downloads folder, ready to email or archive.

All processing happens on the tablet itself — the page images never upload anywhere, which is the detail that matters for contracts, IDs, and medical paperwork. There are no watermarks, no page limits, and no Microsoft account required.

Method 3: scan with the Microsoft 365 Copilot app

If you prefer a Microsoft app, the scanning feature now lives inside the Microsoft 365 Copilot mobile/desktop companion app rather than a standalone scanner. Install it, sign in with your Microsoft account, and use its create/scan entry point to capture documents. Scans flow into OneDrive, which is convenient if your files already live there — though the Copilot scanner currently drops a few conveniences the old Lens app had, like saving straight into OneNote or Word. For quick scans to OneDrive it's fine; for everything else, Methods 1 and 2 are simpler.

A note on Microsoft Lens (the old Office Lens)

If older tutorials send you to Microsoft Lens (formerly Office Lens), don't bother downloading it: Microsoft retired the standalone app in late 2025 — new scans were disabled, and it has been removed from the app stores. Microsoft's official guidance is to use the Copilot app's scanning feature instead. Any guide still listing Lens as its top pick is out of date.

Tips for steadier tablet scans

  • Detach or fold the keyboard first. Trying to aim the rear camera while the Type Cover dangles gets awkward fast — tablet mode exists for this job.
  • Light the page, not the screen. Face a window or a bright lamp; watch that the tablet's own shadow doesn't fall across the page you're capturing.
  • Use a contrasting background. White paper on a dark desk helps every edge-detection algorithm lock on — black table, white page is the classic combo.
  • Stabilise your arms. A 2-in-1 is heavier than a phone; rest your elbows on the table and let the tablet hover, or prop the page on a book so you capture straight down.
  • Mark up with the Surface Pen. After scanning, open the PDF in the built-in PDF viewer or OneNote and use the Pen to sign, highlight, or annotate — no printing required.
  • Name files immediately. "Camera Roll 214.jpg" tells future-you nothing — rename to something like "2026-10-20 Insurance Claim p1.jpg" while the context is fresh.

FAQ

Can I scan documents on a Surface tablet without installing an app?
Yes. Open a browser-based scanner like Scanner.cam in Edge or Chrome, allow camera access, and scan straight to a multi-page PDF — no installs, no accounts, and nothing uploads to a server.

Does the Windows Camera app have a document scan mode?
On some devices, yes — look for a document or scan icon in the Camera app's mode bar. If the button isn't there, your camera doesn't support it; use the browser route instead.

What happened to Microsoft Lens / Office Lens?
Microsoft retired the standalone Lens app in late 2025 and removed it from the app stores. Microsoft now points users to the scanning feature inside the Microsoft 365 Copilot app.

How should I hold a Surface to scan a page?
Detach the keyboard or fold it behind the screen, then hold the tablet with the rear camera facing the desk, parallel to the page. Steady it with both hands and rest your elbows on the table to kill the wobble.

Where do my scans get saved?
The Windows Camera app saves captures to your Pictures folder (Camera Roll). Browser scans download to Downloads as a PDF. Scans made in the Copilot app land in OneDrive.
