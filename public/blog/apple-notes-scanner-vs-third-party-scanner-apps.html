Apple Notes Scanner vs Third-Party Scanner Apps: Which Should You Use?

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 28 Oct 2026 · 6 min read
Quick answer

Apple Notes already scans documents well, so most iPhone owners do not need another app for everyday pages. Third-party scanner apps earn their install only when you need features Notes lacks, like OCR text export, ID-card mode, or Android-friendly files.

The rest of this guide compares the two side by side — capture quality, editing, filing, and cost — so you can decide in two minutes whether Notes is enough or a dedicated app is worth the space on your phone.

What Apple Notes' scanner actually does

On iPhone and iPad, the Notes app has a built-in document scanner. Apple's own support steps are simple: open a note, tap the attachment button, choose Scan Documents, point the camera at the page, and let Auto mode capture it or press the shutter yourself. You then drag the corners to fit the page, tap Keep Scan, add more pages, and tap Save. The result lives inside the note as a scanned document you can mark up, sign, and share — and you can export it as a PDF (Portable Document Format) file from the share menu.

For the most common jobs, that is the whole workflow: a receipt for expenses, a form for school, a page of a contract, a few pages of a lease. Notes handles edge detection, perspective correction, and colour or grayscale filters, and it does all of it with no extra download, no subscription, and no new account — it is part of the phone you already own. If you scan a few pages a week and your scans end up shared by email or message, Notes is genuinely hard to beat.

Where Notes starts to feel limited

Notes is a note-taking app with a scanner inside it, not a scanner app, and the difference shows up as soon as your needs get specific:

  • No OCR text export: Notes does not turn a scan into editable text you can paste into a document or spreadsheet. Search inside Notes can find words in some scans, but you cannot export the recognised text as a Word file.
  • No ID-card or book mode: there is no dedicated layout that puts the front and back of an ID card on one page, or flattens a book spread. You can scan two pages and arrange them yourself, but the app does not do the layout for you.
  • Filing is note-shaped: scans live inside notes. If you manage hundreds of documents — tax years, client files, property paperwork — a note list is a weaker filing cabinet than folders, tags, and batch export.
  • Apple-only: Notes scans are at home on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. If you also use Android or Windows, or you share a device with someone who does, your scans do not travel as easily.
  • No batch or auto-capture workflow: Notes scans one document at a time, by hand. There is no rapid multi-page mode for working through a stack of invoices.

None of these are flaws — they are simply outside what Notes was built to do. They matter only if your scanning goes beyond the occasional page.

What third-party scanner apps add

Dedicated scanner apps — Adobe Scan, Genius Scan, SwiftScan, and browser-based scanners such as Scanner.cam — are built around the scan itself. Depending on the app, you get:

  • OCR and text export: convert a scan into searchable, editable text, and export it to Word, Excel, or plain text.
  • ID and book modes: purpose-built layouts for identity cards, passports, receipts, and book pages.
  • E-signatures and annotation: sign, highlight, and mark up scans inside the app, often with saved signatures you can reuse.
  • Batch capture: rapid multi-page scanning, automatic page separation, and document folders for high-volume work.
  • Cross-platform files: PDFs that open identically on Android, Windows, and the web, and exports straight to Drive, Dropbox, or email.
  • No-install options: browser-based scanners need no download at all — open a page, scan, download the PDF.

The trade is that dedicated apps usually ask for something in return: an install, often an account for cloud sync, and sometimes a subscription for the full feature set. Some free tiers watermark exports or reserve OCR for paying users, so check the free tier before you commit (see our guide to what "free" really costs).

Head-to-head

  • Cost: Notes is free and built in. Dedicated apps range from completely free (browser-based) to freemium with subscriptions for OCR and advanced modes.
  • Account required: Notes needs only your Apple ID, which your iPhone already has. Installed apps often want their own account for sync; browser-based scanners need none at all.
  • Scan quality basics: comparable. Edge detection, perspective correction, filters, and multi-page PDF export are all solid in Notes and in good dedicated apps.
  • OCR and editing: dedicated apps win clearly — text export, ID and book modes, signing, and annotation are their home turf.
  • Finding scans later: dedicated apps win for volume — folders, tags, and search across hundreds of documents beat a note list.
  • Sharing: Notes wins for Apple-to-Apple sharing; dedicated apps win when the recipient is on Android or Windows.
  • Privacy: Notes keeps scans in your iCloud notes by default. Device-local or browser-based scanners that process on your device keep scans off any cloud entirely — check where each app sends your files before scanning anything sensitive.

Which should you use?

  • Stick with Apple Notes if: you scan a few pages a week, you live on Apple devices, and your scans are shared by email or message rather than filed in bulk.
  • Add a third-party app if: you need OCR text export, ID-card or book layouts, e-signatures, or a filing system for hundreds of documents.
  • Use a browser-based scanner if: you want no install and no account — for example on a borrowed phone, a shared computer, or for a sensitive document you would rather never upload to a cloud.
  • Use both if: your needs split. Many iPhone owners scan everyday pages in Notes and keep a dedicated scanner for the jobs Notes cannot do. There is no rule that says one tool must do everything.

The deciding question is not which scanner is "best" — it is what happens to the scan after you take it. If the answer is "it goes in a note and gets shared," Notes is already the right tool and already installed. If the answer is "I need editable text, an ID layout, or a clean file on any device," that is exactly the gap third-party apps exist to fill.

Can Apple Notes scan documents?
Yes. Open a note, tap the attachment button, choose Scan Documents, capture each page, adjust the corners, and tap Save. The scan lives in the note and can be marked up, signed, and shared or exported as a PDF.

Is the Notes scanner free?
Yes. It is built into iOS and iPadOS at no extra cost, with no watermark on exported scans and no subscription for scanning itself.

Can Notes export OCR text?
No. Notes does not export recognised text into an editable Word or text file. For OCR text export, use a dedicated scanner app with an OCR feature or a browser-based scanner that offers it.

Can I scan an ID card with Notes?
You can scan both sides as two pages, but Notes has no dedicated ID-card mode that lays front and back out on one page automatically. Dedicated scanner apps with an ID mode do that layout for you.

Should I delete my scanner app if I have Notes?
Only if Notes covers everything you scan. If you use OCR export, ID mode, signing, or batch filing even once a month, keep the dedicated app for those jobs and let Notes handle the everyday pages.
