Can I Delete My Scans From a Scanner App's Cloud? A Step-by-Step Guide

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 30 Oct 2026 · 6 min read
Quick answer

Yes, you can usually delete your scans from a scanner app's cloud — but deleting the app from your phone does not do it. You need to delete the scans inside your cloud account, empty any trash folder, revoke shared links, and delete the account itself if you want everything gone.

This is the detail most people miss. When a scanner app uploads your documents, there are suddenly three copies to think about: the copy on your phone, the copy in the app's cloud, and any copies you shared or that a backup made. Removing only the phone copy leaves the cloud copy sitting on someone else's server. This guide walks through each copy, in order, so nothing is left behind by accident.

Understand the three copies first

Start by mapping where your scans actually live. Open the scanner app and check whether it has an account or a cloud library — if it does, your scans may be synced there even if you thought you were saving locally. Check your phone's photo library or files app too, since some apps save a copy there as well. Finally, think about sharing: did you email a scan, send a link, or save one to another cloud service such as Google Drive or Dropbox? Each of those is a separate copy with its own delete step.

Knowing the three copies before you start deleting saves you from the most common mistake: uninstalling the app in frustration and assuming the job is done, while the cloud account quietly keeps everything.

Step 1: Delete the scans inside the app

Open the scanner app while you are still signed in, and delete the documents from its library. Work through folders and tags, not just the main list — old scans often hide in an archive or an "all documents" view. If the app lets you select everything at once, use that; if not, delete in batches and double-check the count before moving on.

Pay attention to any "recently deleted" or trash folder. Many apps keep deleted items for days or weeks before removing them permanently, which means a scan you deleted today may still be recoverable — by you, and by anyone with access to the account — until that period ends. Empty the trash manually if the app allows it.

Step 2: Revoke shared links and shared folders

A deleted scan can still be reachable through a link you shared earlier, if the app keeps shared links live separately from the document itself. Go to the app's sharing settings, if it has them, and revoke or expire any links you created for the documents you are removing. If you sent a scan by email or message, remember that the recipient now has their own copy — you cannot delete that from your side, so only share sensitive scans when you have to, and prefer sending the file yourself rather than a cloud link.

Step 3: Delete the account if you are done with the app

If you no longer use the app, deleting your account is the strongest step available. Most reputable apps offer account deletion in their settings or on their website, and privacy laws in some regions require them to offer it. Deleting the account should remove your stored documents along with it, though the app's privacy policy may state a retention period for backups — read that section so you know what "deleted" means for that service.

Before you delete the account, export anything you still need. Download the scans you want to keep to your own device first, because account deletion is usually irreversible and support teams cannot always restore what you removed.

Step 4: Check device copies and backups

After the cloud is clean, check your phone. Delete local copies from the app's folder, your photo library, and your downloads, and check whether your phone's own cloud backup has been backing up the app's data. On both iPhone and Android you can exclude an app from cloud backup in settings, and you can delete its backed-up data if it is already there. This step is easy to forget, and it is how "deleted" scans quietly survive on a new phone after a restore.

Finally, sign out everywhere. If you were signed into the scanner app on a tablet or an old phone, sign out on those devices too, so a forgotten login cannot re-sync documents you just removed.

How long does deletion really take?

Honest answer: it varies. Some services remove your data immediately, others keep it in backups for a period stated in their privacy policy, and shared copies are outside your control entirely. That is why the order matters — delete the live copies and the links first, then the account, then the backups — and why the best protection for sensitive documents is not uploading them in the first place.

Going forward, the simplest habit is to scan in your browser with a tool that never uploads your pages. Scanner.cam processes everything on your device, so there is no cloud copy to chase down later: you download the PDF, and that is the only copy that exists.

Frequently asked questions

Does deleting a scanner app delete my scans?
No, not from the cloud. Uninstalling removes the app from your phone, but scans stored in the app's cloud account stay there until you delete them inside the account or delete the account itself.

How long do deleted scans stay in backups?
It depends on the service. Some remove data immediately; others state a backup-retention period in their privacy policy. Check that policy for the specific app, and empty its trash folder manually to shorten the window.

Can I delete one scan without deleting everything?
Yes, in most apps. Delete the individual document from the app's library, empty the trash, and revoke any link you shared for that document. You only need full account deletion if you want everything gone.

What about scans I already shared with someone?
You can revoke a shared link, but you cannot delete a file from someone else's inbox or device. For sensitive documents, prefer sending the file directly and only to people who need it.

Do I have a legal right to deletion?
In some regions, yes — privacy laws such as the GDPR in Europe and the CCPA in California give residents rights to request deletion of their personal data, with exceptions. The exact right depends on where you live and on the service's terms, so check the app's privacy page for its deletion-request process.
