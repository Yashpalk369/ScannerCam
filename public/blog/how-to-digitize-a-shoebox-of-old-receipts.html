How to Digitize a Shoebox of Old Receipts

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 3 Nov 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

To digitize a shoebox of old receipts, sort them by year, scan them in short batches of twenty or so, and name each file by date and shop as you go. Working in small batches keeps the project finishable, and date-first names make any receipt findable later without opening files one by one.

A shoebox that looks like a weekend of misery is usually two or three evenings of steady scanning.

The shoebox is the classic household archive: years of receipts tipped into a box "just in case", now faded, crumpled, and impossible to search. The goal is not a perfect archive — it is a searchable one. You do not need to scan every slip, and you do not need to do it in one sitting. You need a simple pipeline you can repeat for twenty minutes at a time until the box is empty.

Sort before you scan

Tip the box out and make quick piles by year, using the printed date on each slip. Do not read every receipt — just glance at the year and move on. Pull out anything obviously useless (a coffee receipt from five years ago with no expense or warranty attached) and anything damaged beyond reading. What remains is your scanning queue, and sorting first means you will never scan the same year twice or wonder later which pile a file came from.

Scan in batches of twenty

  1. Take one year-pile and one batch of about twenty receipts — small enough to finish in one sitting.
  2. Flatten curled slips under a book for a few minutes while you scan the flat ones; work through the pile in date order if you can.
  3. Scan each receipt with Scanner.cam in your phone's browser: lay it flat, use even light, skip the flash on glossy thermal slips, and check the total and date are readable before moving on.
  4. Save the batch as one multi-page PDF per year, or as individual files if you prefer — the key is consistency, so pick one and stick with it for the whole box.
  5. After each batch, move the scanned paper into a "done" envelope so you never rescan the same slip.

Name and file so you can find things later

If you scan individual files, name them date first, then shop, then amount if it matters: 2023-06-14-hardware-store.pdf. If you scan one PDF per year, keep a short index note — a plain text file listing the notable receipts in each year's PDF and the page they sit on. Either way, store the finished scans in one folder per year inside a receipts folder, and copy the whole folder to a second place (cloud drive or external disk) once the box is done. Two copies is the difference between an archive and a gamble.

What to do with the paper afterwards

Once a batch is scanned, checked, and backed up, the paper has done its job for everyday purposes. Keep originals that might still be needed in person — warranty slips for items still covered, or receipts tied to an open claim — in one labelled envelope. The rest can be shredded or recycled. If you are unsure about a particular receipt, keep it: the envelope costs nothing, while a missing receipt at claim time costs real money.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a shoebox take to digitize?
A typical shoebox of a few hundred receipts takes two to three evenings at twenty minutes a session. Sorting by year first is what keeps it from feeling endless.

Should I scan every receipt or just the important ones?
Scan anything tied to money you might need to prove: warranties, large purchases, business expenses, and tax items. Everyday small purchases with no claim attached can be skipped without regret.

One PDF per year or one file per receipt?
One PDF per year is faster and easier to back up; individual files are easier to search by name. For a shoebox, one PDF per year plus a short index note is usually the best balance.

What about faded thermal receipts?
Scan those first, before the rest of the box — they are the ones still losing print. Use even light, no flash, and raise the contrast after capture to pull faint text back.

Do I need a scanner app for this?
No. A browser scanner does the whole job: capture, straighten, and export to PDF, with no install and no account. That keeps a long project free of subscription friction.
