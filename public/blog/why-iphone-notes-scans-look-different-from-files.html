Why Does My iPhone Scan in Notes Look Different from Files?

By Scanner.cam Team · Updated 3 Nov 2026 · 4 min read
Quick answer

Your iPhone scan looks different in Notes and Files mainly because of where the file ends up and which default filter each app applies. The camera capture itself is the same scanner in both apps, so the difference is in processing and presentation, not in the camera.

Notes keeps the scan inside a note with its own editing options, while Files saves a plain PDF into a folder.

If you have scanned the same page twice — once from Notes, once from Files — and the two results did not match, you are not imagining it. Both apps open the same Apple document scanner, but they treat the result differently: different default filters, different file handling, and different options for what happens next. None of that means one of them is broken.

The capture is the same; the handling is not

Open Scan Documents in Notes and in Files and you will see the same viewfinder: automatic edge detection, flash and filter controls, and the option to keep adding pages. The difference starts after you tap Save. Notes stores the scan as a PDF attached inside a note, where you can mark it up, sign it, or share the whole note. Files drops a standalone PDF into the folder you chose, ready to upload, attach, or file away like any other document.

Why the two scans can look different

  • Filters. Each scan offers colour, grayscale, black-and-white, and photo filters, and the last filter you used tends to stick. If your Notes scan looks punchier and your Files scan looks flat, you probably scanned one with a contrast filter and the other without.
  • Where you view it. A scan viewed inside a note is shown at the note's display size and rendering, while the same PDF opened in Files (or Preview) renders at full document size. Zoom and display differences alone can make one look sharper than the other.
  • What happens on sharing. Sharing from Notes can send the note or a PDF export depending on the option you pick, and some share routes compress the file. Sharing the Files PDF sends the file itself, unchanged.
  • Edits after the fact. Notes lets you rotate, crop, and re-filter scanned pages from inside the note; Files gives you the PDF as-is, and edits need a separate app. A scan you touched up in Notes will naturally look different from the untouched Files original.

Which app should you use?

Use Notes when the scan belongs with a note — a receipt you want to annotate, a form you need to sign, or pages you want to keep searchable alongside typed notes. Use Files when the scan is a standalone document: a tax paper for a folder, an application upload, or anything you will attach to an email or a portal. If you need the scan in both places, scan once in Files, then share the PDF into a note — that way the file itself stays identical.

How to make the two match

  1. Set the same filter before scanning in both apps — colour for documents with stamps or photos, grayscale or black-and-white for plain text.
  2. Turn flash off for glossy paper and on for dim rooms, in both apps, so lighting does not become the hidden variable.
  3. Compare the files in the same viewer: open both PDFs in Files or Preview rather than judging one inside a note and the other as a file.
  4. If one scan still looks off, rescan that page with the filter you want rather than trying to fix a finished PDF.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Notes scanner lower quality than the Files scanner?
No. Both use the same built-in document scanner and the same camera. Visible differences come from filters, viewing size, and sharing routes, not from capture quality.

Can I move a Notes scan into Files?
Yes. Open the scan in the note, tap the share button, and choose Save to Files. You get the same PDF as a standalone file in the folder you pick.

Why does my Notes scan look darker?
Most likely the black-and-white or grayscale filter was active when you scanned, or the page was viewed at a smaller size. Reopen the scan, check the filter setting, and switch it back to colour if the original had colour content.

Does scanning in Files save to iCloud automatically?
It saves to the folder you choose — iCloud Drive if that folder is in iCloud, or On My iPhone if you picked local storage. Check the folder path if a scan seems to have vanished.

Which one is better for scanning without any app at all?
Both are built in, so neither needs a download. If you want no note and no folder fuss, scan in a browser instead: open Scanner.cam, capture, and download the PDF directly.
