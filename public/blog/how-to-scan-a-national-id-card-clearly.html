How to Scan a National ID Card (CNIC-Style) Clearly

By Scanner.cam Editorial Team · Updated 6 Nov 2026 · 5 min read
Quick answer

National ID cards are small, stiff, and glossy, which is why phone photos of them come out blurry or full of glare. Scan your CNIC-style card on a dark matte background, hold the camera steady at close range, and put both sides on one PDF page.

A national ID card — a CNIC in Pakistan, an Aadhaar card in India, a national identity card anywhere else — is the document most often asked for in online verification: bank accounts, SIM registration, government portals, and freelance platforms all want a copy. The card itself fights back: it is barely bigger than a credit card, printed on glossy plastic, and covered in small text, a photo, and security patterns. The technique below is built for exactly that — small, stiff, reflective cards — rather than for full-size paper documents.

Scan a national ID card in 6 steps

  1. Wipe the card clean. Fingerprints on glossy plastic scatter light. A quick wipe with a soft, dry cloth makes a visible difference.
  2. Lay it on a dark, matte background. Dark paper or a dark desk gives the camera something to lock onto, and a matte surface will not throw reflections back at the lens.
  3. Use daylight or a lamp to the side. Never use flash on a glossy card. Light coming from the side at a low angle keeps the text readable and the glare off the photo.
  4. Hold the camera close and steady. Fill the frame with the card plus a small margin. Rest your elbows on the table or prop the phone — at this distance, even a small shake blurs the small print.
  5. Scan the front, then flip and scan the back. Check each side at full zoom before moving on: the name, number, photo, and expiry date must all be sharp.
  6. Put both sides on one PDF page. Front on top, back below, in a single file. That is the format most verification portals expect, and it keeps the two sides from getting separated.

Why small cards scan badly

Three things work against you with a national ID card. First, the size: the text is printed for a card you hold in your hand, not for a photo taken from arm's length, so the camera needs to be close — and close means shallow focus and visible shake. Second, the gloss: laminated plastic reflects any light source straight into the lens, which is why the photo or the card number so often disappears into a white patch. Third, the contrast: a light-coloured card on a light table gives the camera no edge to find, so it crops the card wrong or leaves a grey border. Fixing all three is simple — dark background, side light, steady close camera — but skipping any one of them is usually what produces a rejected scan.

Both sides on one page

Most portals ask for the front and back of your ID in a single file. The cleanest way to do that is a two-page PDF — front first, back second — or a single page with both sides stacked. Either is accepted by most systems; what matters is that both sides are present, in order, and readable. Do not shrink the card images to fit both on one page if it makes the text unreadable — a two-page PDF at full size is better than a one-page PDF nobody can read. Name the file after the document, for example cnic-front-back-2026-11-06.pdf, so you can find it again without opening it.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Scanning on a white table. A pale card on a pale surface confuses edge detection and washes out the borders. Use a dark background.
  • Using flash. Flash and glossy plastic do not mix. Turn it off and use a lamp or window light from the side.
  • Holding the phone too far away. If the card fills less than half the frame, the small text will not survive. Get close and hold steady.
  • Cutting off a corner. Verification systems check the card's outline. A cropped corner or a finger over the edge reads as a damaged or altered document.
  • Sending the scan anywhere unsecured. Your national ID number is the key to your identity. Upload it only through the portal that asked for it — never by email, chat, or a file-sharing link.

Frequently asked questions

How do I scan a CNIC without glare?
Turn off the flash, put the card on a dark matte surface, and light it from the side — a lamp or a window — instead of from above. If a reflection still sits on the photo, tilt the card a few degrees until it moves away.

Should the front and back be in one file or two?
One file, front first and back second, is what most verification portals expect. A two-page PDF is the safest format.

Why is the small text on my ID scan blurry?
Usually the camera was too far away or moved during the shot. Get closer, rest your arms on the table, and check the text at full zoom before you upload.

What size should an ID card scan be?
Aim for a PDF under 5 MB that still keeps the text sharp. If the portal states a smaller limit, match it — but never shrink the scan so far that the numbers become unreadable.

Is it safe to scan my national ID card online?
It is safe only through a trusted service's own secure upload, using a tool that processes the scan on your device. Never send your ID by unencrypted email or upload it to a site you have not checked first.
